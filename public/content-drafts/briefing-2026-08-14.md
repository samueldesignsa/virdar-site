5. **Referral Capture:** Men who get results tell their friends. The 90-day check-in with a referral link captures that conversation and gives the friend a direct path to book. **3.3 additional referral-attributed new patients/month = $1,287 additional monthly recurring revenue from patients already on protocol.**

## One Insight

Today's men's health blog surfaces something worth noting for Beverly's pitch repertoire: the embarrassment gap is a customer acquisition problem specific to certain healthcare categories, and it has a direct analogue in nearly every sensitive service business.

Men who want TRT don't call. They submit forms anonymously at night. Men who want ED treatment don't call the front desk of a clinic. They go online. The same dynamic applies to any business where the customer has a reluctance to make first contact: weight loss clinics (the woman who hasn't weighed herself in two years), mental health practices (the person who needs therapy but doesn't want to say it out loud), men's health (obvious), even financial advisors (the family that knows their finances are a mess and is embarrassed about it).

For all of these categories, the solution is the same: respond immediately when they reach out digitally, make the first step completely frictionless and private, and don't make them call you. The businesses that understand this design their entire first-contact experience around the customer's reluctance. The ones that don't are competing with businesses that do.

Beverly can use this in any healthcare or sensitive services stop today: "When someone reaches out to your business for the first time, what's the experience like? Is it easy to take that first step without making a phone call? Because for a lot of your potential patients, the first time they contact you is the hardest thing they do that week. If the process requires them to call, and calling is hard, some percentage of them just don't do it."
