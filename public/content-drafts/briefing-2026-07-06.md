## One Insight

The pattern across the last 10 blog posts — dance studios, music schools, gymnastics studios, nail salons, now pediatricians — is a single repeating structure: the owner knows her patients/clients personally, she can name the ones who haven't been in recently, and she has no system that acts on that knowledge. The knowledge exists. The action doesn't.

Pediatric practices are the clearest version of this. A pediatrician knows which kids play football, which family just moved in from out of state, which patient is on ADHD medication that needs a quarterly review. That knowledge is in the chart. The chart doesn't reach out. The family doesn't call until they need something. The practice doesn't capture the visit until the need creates urgency — and urgency often means urgent care, not the practice.

The back-to-school season is the moment when the gap between "the practice knows" and "the family knows they should come in" is most expensive. Every week of July that passes without a recall message is a week of July appointments that become August pile-up. The practices Beverly talks to this week aren't choosing between having a system and not having one — they're choosing between building one now and scrambling in August.
