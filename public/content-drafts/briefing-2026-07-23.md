### 1. Blog Post #64 — AI Automation for Concierge Medicine / Direct Primary Care
**File:** `public/blog/ai-automation-for-concierge-medicine-direct-primary-care.html`

This is the first post in the library covering the DPC and concierge medicine model — which operates entirely differently from fee-for-service practices. The automation problems here are membership-layer problems, not appointment-layer problems. Every revenue number is annualized because the model is recurring.

**Five specific problems with revenue math:**

1. **Enrollment → First Visit (30-day window):** New members who don't complete a first visit within 30 days cancel at 3× the rate of those who do. Automated onboarding sequence — welcome message + scheduling prompt within 1 hour of enrollment — lifts 30-day first-visit completion from 55% to 85%. **3 additional first visits/month × 65% improved year-1 retention = 2 additional members retained through renewal × $1,548 annual value = $37,152 annualized.**

2. **Annual Renewal Attrition (Month 10 problem):** Members approaching renewal without recent contact make a quiet cancellation decision. A month-10 value summary — listing every touchpoint, confirming upcoming access — drops renewal cancellation from 9% to 5%. **13 members retained × $1,548 = $20,124/year from a message that runs automatically on each member's 10-month anniversary.**

3. **Low Utilization = Pre-Cancellation Signal:** Members who use 1–2 visits per year feel the least value and cancel at the highest rate. Automated quarterly check-in ("Is there anything you'd like to address in the next few weeks?") — 35% response rate, 18% visit conversion — drives engagement before non-use becomes a cancellation rationale. **13 additional members retained annually × $1,548 = $20,124/year.**

4. **Referral Engine:** High-satisfaction care moments (same-day acute visit, after-hours text response, fast prescription renewal) generate the best referrals — but only if the practice asks within 24 hours while the experience is vivid. Automated post-visit referral prompt drives 1.5–2 new members per month from existing members. **$27,864 to $37,152 in new annual recurring revenue from the referral system alone.**

5. **Waitlist Management:** Open spots that take 22 days to fill should take 48 hours. Automated same-day waitlist outreach to the top 3 candidates with a short confirmation window recovers ~20 days of revenue per open spot. **$6,450/year for a practice with 2–3 monthly churn events.**

Post ends with a clear distinction: DPC automation is administrative (scheduling, communication, retention), never clinical. The physician relationship stays intact.

Today's blog introduces the DPC model for the first time in the library. It's the most distinct post from everything else published because the automation logic is fundamentally different — everything in DPC is membership-layer, not appointment-layer. Fee-for-service practices lose money to missed appointments and unreturned calls. DPC practices lose money to membership attrition and utilization gaps.

The most important thing for Beverly to understand about the DPC conversation: the physician who runs a DPC practice is not looking to get more patients. They capped enrollment deliberately. They are running a quality-of-care model, not a volume model. The automation pitch that works for a dentist ("reach your overdue patients before they call someone else") doesn't land the same way. The DPC pitch is: "You already have the members. The automation keeps them." That reframe — retention, not acquisition — is what opens the door.

1. "How long have you been open?" (Establish timeline — 2–4 year DPC practices are in the first renewal cycle)

3. "The practices that know that number and actively manage the month-10 retention window keep 30% more of the at-risk cohort. Here's what that looks like for a 320-member practice." (Show today's blog — the $20,124 math is the specific anchor)

That three-step sequence assumes nothing and surfaces the problem the physician didn't know they had a metric for. It's the same structure as every other Beverly conversation — named problem, specific action, specific revenue math — applied to a model she hasn't had a leave-behind for until today.
