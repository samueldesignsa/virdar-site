# Walk-In Research — Tuesday, September 15, 2026
## Territory: Garland / Mesquite / Balch Springs

East Dallas County. Dense, working-class, and completely off the recent walk-in rotation. Garland is one of the largest cities in DFW by population, but it rarely shows up in conversations about where businesses are getting pitched on AI or automation tools. That gap is the advantage here.

The commercial corridors here are different from north Dallas strip centers. This market has standalone service businesses — auto shops, HVAC companies, pest control, plumbing — interspersed with small professional offices and mid-block strip retail. These owners are running their businesses at full capacity. Most have never heard a specific, concrete pitch about automating their customer follow-up. The ones who have heard "AI automation" have heard it from someone who didn't understand their business well enough to explain what it actually does for them.

**Suggested route:** Garland (Garland Road / Northwest Highway corridor near downtown Garland) 9:30–11am → Mesquite (Town East Boulevard / Gus Thomasson Road strip) 11:15am–12:45pm → Balch Springs (I-20 service road / Elam Road commercial area) 1–2:30pm.

**Entry line for today's territory:** "I work with owner-operated businesses in east Dallas County. Most of the ones I've talked to here are doing strong work and have loyal customers — but nothing is actively working the customer list between jobs. In the fall, that's expensive because the booking window is open and people are making decisions. What does your follow-up to past customers look like right now?"

## Target Businesses

### 1. Independent HVAC Contractor — Garland Road or Belt Line area, Garland
**What they do:** Residential HVAC installation and service in the Garland / Rowlett / Sachse corridor. Owner-operated companies here typically run 2-5 techs and have been in business 10-20 years. Their customer base is almost entirely repeat and referral.
**What to notice:** Fall tune-up season is live right now. Every homeowner in this market who had summer service is statistically overdue for a fall system check before heating season. More importantly, any customer who had a system installed in 2021, 2022, or 2023 and hasn't been converted to a maintenance agreement is a direct recurring revenue opportunity that no one has touched. These customers are warm — they trust the company — but there's no automated system reminding them they're due for maintenance.
**Hook:** "Your install customers from 2022 and 2023 just hit their third or fourth heating season. How many are on a maintenance agreement, and how many only call when the heater doesn't start in November? The gap between those two groups is text messages. Is anything reaching out to your install customer list in September before they search Google when something breaks?"

### 2. Auto Repair or Body Shop — Northwest Highway or Garland Road, Garland
**What they do:** General auto repair, brake service, oil changes, and collision work for the Garland residential market. Independent shops in this market have a loyal customer base but compete directly with national chains on every service road in Dallas County.
**What to notice:** An independent shop's competitive advantage over Jiffy Lube or Midas is relationship — the owner knows the customer's car. But that relationship is invisible if the customer goes 8 months without hearing from the shop. An oil change customer from January hasn't been back. They're not gone — they just didn't think of it. A text reminder at the 4-month mark brings them back before they pull into the chain next door.
**Hook:** "Your oil change customers from April and May — how many of them have been back? The ones who haven't aren't loyal to a chain. They just haven't had a reason to call you. A text message at four months saying 'your vehicle is likely due for service' brings back 15-20% of them that same week. Is anything sending that message, or are you waiting for them to remember?"

**What they do:** General and family dentistry in the Garland market, serving working-class to middle-income families with employer-sponsored dental insurance. Garland has a large working-class commuter population — many of whom have dental benefits through employers they rarely use.
**What to notice:** December 31 dental benefit expiration is 107 days out from today. A family practice in Garland that hasn't started proactive outreach to patients with unused annual benefits will lose that revenue — not to a competitor, just to inertia. The patients won't think about it until late November when their calendar is already full.
**Hook:** "Your patients have dental benefits expiring December 31 — 107 days from today. The patients who haven't been in this year still have coverage. If they don't hear from your office in September, they'll remember in November when you're full. A text to your active patient list that says 'you have benefits expiring December 31 — let's get you scheduled' generates appointments this week. What does your outreach to those patients look like right now?"

### 4. Pest Control Company — Multiple locations, Garland / Mesquite
**What they do:** Residential and commercial pest control, quarterly service agreements, one-time treatments. The Garland and Mesquite market has an older housing stock — lots of 1970s and 1980s homes with established pest pressure — and a residential density that makes route efficiency easy.
**What to notice:** This is the single best reactivation window of the year for pest control. As September temperatures start dropping, rodents and insects begin moving toward structure — which means lapsed accounts from spring are about to feel the consequence of having no service. A pest control owner who reaches out to their lapsed spring customers right now with "fall is when rodents start moving inside — your property is overdue for service" will generate inbound calls this week.
**Hook:** "How many of your spring customers didn't schedule summer service? Those accounts are going into fall rodent season without active coverage. A text to that lapsed list right now generates calls because the timing is perfect — the customers already know what happens when they skip service in the fall. Is anything working that list, or are you waiting for them to call when they find a mouse in November?"

### 5. Plumbing Contractor — Mesquite or Garland, standalone or light industrial
**What they do:** Residential plumbing service and repair, water heater replacement, drain cleaning. Owner-operated plumbing companies in this market run small crews, rely heavily on repeat customers and Google reviews, and almost never do formal follow-up.
**What to notice:** A plumbing company with several years of customer history has a gold mine of homeowners who had service and haven't called back because nothing has broken yet. But fall is when water heaters start failing — the thermal stress of heating up from summer temperatures accelerates failures in aging units. A plumber who reaches out in September to customers who had hot water issues or who have water heaters over 10 years old can book replacement calls proactively.
**Hook:** "Do you know which of your past customers have water heaters over 10 years old? Fall is when they start failing because of thermal stress. A message to those customers — 'your water heater is at high risk of failure this winter, we can replace it before it becomes an emergency' — books jobs that would otherwise go to whoever answers Google at 7am on a Saturday. Is anything working that list, or are those jobs going to whoever's first in the search results when the tank fails?"

## Terrain Note

Garland is underestimated as a walk-in market. It's the 12th largest city in Texas and has a massive concentration of owner-operated service businesses, but it's almost never in the conversation about DFW B2B outreach. The Garland Road corridor near downtown Garland has a mix of standalone service businesses and medical/professional strip buildings. Northwest Highway (IH-635 Business) through Garland has auto shops, HVAC companies, and light commercial services.

Mesquite's best corridor is Town East Boulevard between US-80 and LBJ Freeway — strip centers with independent retailers, auto shops, and professional services that are easy to walk. Gus Thomasson Road has a similar profile.

Balch Springs is smaller and more industrial — the I-20 service road has the concentration, and it's worth 2-3 targeted stops rather than a long canvass.

Drive time Garland → Mesquite: 15 minutes. Mesquite → Balch Springs: 10 minutes. No traffic issues mid-morning on a Tuesday.
