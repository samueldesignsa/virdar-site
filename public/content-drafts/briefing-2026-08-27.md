## One Insight

**The credibility-resistant buyer is the best buyer.**

The Southlake/Colleyville market is different from the South DFW markets Beverly's been working. These owners have money, they've made good decisions, and they've been pitched by every tech vendor in the metroplex. They didn't get to where they are by buying things that didn't work.

That resistance is actually an advantage for Virdar. The pitch is: "You've probably seen a lot of vendors come through with promises they couldn't keep. Here's what's different: you don't pay until you see it running on your actual workflow." That lands differently when the buyer has been burned before.

The businesses Beverly should close are the ones who asked good questions during the cold walk-in. The ones who immediately said yes without asking anything are lower-quality prospects. Skepticism is a good sign in a high-income market — it means they're evaluating seriously rather than dismissing.

**The walk-in principle for affluent markets:** Match the buyer's seriousness. Don't oversell. Ask questions and let them tell you what's broken. "What does your September look like right now?" is a better opener than any pitch, because it gives them something to complain about — and the complaint is the sale.
