## One Insight

Rockwall County's growth pattern creates a specific type of client that Beverly should look for today: the independent practice that opened 4–6 years ago with a patient panel of 80 people and now has 350, but is still running on the same infrastructure it had at 80. The front desk process, the recall system, the appointment reminder cadence — all designed for a small practice that could manage patient relationships manually. At 350 patients, manual is already breaking. At 500, it collapses.

The business owner in that situation often knows something is wrong — they're busier than ever but losing patients they didn't know they were losing, their reviews are flat, and their referral rate isn't keeping up with their growth. But they haven't diagnosed it as a systems problem. They think they need to hire someone.

The conversation Beverly can open with today: "You've been in Rockwall for five or six years, right? This county grew fast. Did your back-office infrastructure keep up with that growth, or are you running the same systems you had when you had half the patients you have now?" Most owners pause at that question. The pause is the opening.
