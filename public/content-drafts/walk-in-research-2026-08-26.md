# Walk-In Research — Wednesday, August 26, 2026
## Territory: Carrollton / Farmers Branch / Addison

**Why this territory today:**
Dense commercial corridor between Dallas and Plano — Carrollton is one of the largest cities in DFW by population and heavily undervisited by tech vendors. Farmers Branch has a compact, longtime-owner business district along Belt Line Road. Addison is affluent and service-rich — concentrated medical, dental, fitness, and beauty businesses in a walkable stretch along Addison Road and Belt Line. The Carrollton/Farmers Branch business owners tend to be longer-tenured (15–25 years in the same location) and more skeptical of vendors than Frisco or McKinney. Addison skews slightly younger-owner, higher-revenue per business, and more open to systems conversations.

**Framing for this market:** Lead with a specific dollar number and a specific problem. "Your September HVAC appointments — are you filling them from your maintenance list, or waiting for inbounds?" is better than "we help businesses communicate better." In Addison, you can go slightly more concept-first with high-earning service businesses. In Carrollton, stay concrete.

**Drive time from central DFW:** 20–30 minutes north/northwest.

## 10 Targets

### 1. Carrollton Family Dentistry / Old Bent Tree Dental (Carrollton)
**What they do:** General family dentistry, likely 2–3 providers, treating families who have been patients for 10–15 years alongside new residents from recent Carrollton growth.

**Specific observation:** September is the final window for back-to-school dental recalls that started in July. Any patient who had a cleaning in August or September 2025 is overdue if they haven't been reached with a specific message and a direct link to book. The practices that survived the back-to-school crunch are the ones who started recall outreach in mid-July. Anyone who didn't is now scrambling for September slots against competition from corporate dental chains in the same zip codes.

**Hook:** "Your patients who had their last cleaning in September 2025 are due right now. How many of them got a specific text or email with a direct link to book, versus a postcard that may or may not have arrived? The ones you haven't heard from in the last 30 days — who's following up with them?"

### 2. Addison Dermatology / DFW Derm (Addison, TX 75001)
**What they do:** Independent or small-group dermatology — cosmetic and medical, treating patients in an affluent zip code with high disposable income. Mix of Botox/filler patients, acne, skin cancer screenings, prescription skincare.

**Specific observation:** September is the start of "injectable season" — cooler weather, fall events, holiday prep. Cosmetic derm patients who had their last Botox treatment 4–5 months ago are at the natural re-treatment window. The practice that reaches those patients with "It's been about four months since your last treatment — your result is likely starting to soften. We have September openings" wins the appointment before the patient books somewhere else.

**Hook:** "Your Botox patients from April — they're at the four-to-five month mark right now. The ones who haven't rebooked, how are they hearing from you? A message that says 'your result is softening, here are September openings' converts at a completely different rate than a generic newsletter."

### 3. Farmers Branch Chiropractic / Belt Line Chiro (Farmers Branch)
**What they do:** Independent chiropractic, likely serving a mix of cash-pay maintenance patients, auto injury, and insurance-covered treatment. Belt Line corridor has several longtime independent practices.

**Specific observation:** Q4 benefit deadline is the strongest revenue hook for chiropractic right now. Patients who completed active care earlier this year still have insurance or FSA/HSA coverage that expires December 31. The practice that reaches them in September with "You have coverage remaining through December — this is a good time to schedule maintenance care" fills the fall schedule. Most practices don't run this outreach.

**Hook:** "Your patients from the first half of this year who have remaining insurance or FSA benefits — they expire December 31. Do they know? The practice that reaches them in September with that message fills October and November. What does your fall outreach look like?"

### 4. Addison Aesthetics / Medical Spa (Addison)
**What they do:** Medical spa or aesthetics practice in an affluent zip code. Likely offering laser treatments, injectables, body contouring, skin resurfacing, and memberships.

**Specific observation:** Laser and skin resurfacing treatments are best done in fall and winter when sun exposure is lower. September is the natural marketing window for procedures that patients have been deferring all summer. A lapsed client list — members who haven't visited in 3–6 months — is a high-value asset that most independent med spas are not actively working.

**Hook:** "Your lapsed clients — the ones who came in for a treatment or a series 6 months ago and haven't booked since — how many names is that? The fall is the best time of year to reach them with 'now is the perfect time for [treatment they had].' That campaign costs almost nothing and is usually the highest-ROI outreach an aesthetics practice runs all year."

### 5. Carrollton Veterinary Hospital / Animal Hospital of Carrollton
**What they do:** Mixed small animal practice — dogs, cats, small mammals. Likely 2–4 DVMs serving longtime Carrollton families and newer residents from surrounding subdivisions.

**Specific observation:** Annual wellness recall is the backbone of vet practice revenue. September and October wellness visits are driven by two cohorts: pets due on their annual schedule (vaccine due dates fall year-round) and pets adopted in spring/summer who need their first adult wellness visit. The practice that proactively reaches both with a specific message and a direct booking link captures those appointments. The one that waits for clients to call loses some to corporate chains with better online scheduling.

**Hook:** "Your wellness-due patients this month — they're in the system. The ones you haven't seen in 13+ months, are they getting a specific message with a link to book? Or are you waiting for them to call? The clients who got a text saying 'Bella's annual vaccines are due this month — click here to pick a time' convert at about three times the rate of a generic postcard."

### 6. North Carrollton Physical Therapy / Carrollton PT (Carrollton, TX 75006)
**What they do:** Outpatient orthopedic PT, treating a mix of sports injuries, post-surgical, and chronic musculoskeletal conditions. Likely receiving referrals from orthopedic surgeons, family medicine, and sports medicine practices in the area.

**Specific observation:** Referral response speed is the single biggest revenue variable for independent PT. A physician's office faxes a referral at 3pm Friday. The PT clinic calls Monday morning. The patient, who is in pain and wants to start immediately, has already called a larger clinic that answered their call over the weekend and scheduled them for Monday. The independent PT who doesn't call until Monday never knows they lost the patient.

**Hook:** "When a referral comes in after 4pm on a Friday, when's the first call that patient gets from your office? The practice that reaches out within two hours — even with an automated message that says 'we received your referral and will be calling to schedule' — books that patient at a significantly higher rate than the one that calls back Monday morning."

### 7. Addison Dentistry / Cosmetic Dental Addison
**What they do:** Cosmetic and general dentistry in an affluent suburb — Addison-area practices often see higher case acceptance for elective work (veneers, whitening, Invisalign) and higher average ticket per patient.

**Specific observation:** Treatment plan dropout is expensive in cosmetic dentistry. A patient who accepted an Invisalign case or a veneer plan in the spring and then went quiet over summer needs a specific re-engagement message — not a generic newsletter, a direct note that references their plan. September is the natural re-entry window (back to routine, school starting, feeling ready to invest in themselves again).

**Hook:** "Your patients who accepted a treatment plan in the spring — Invisalign, veneers, full-mouth restoration — and went quiet over summer. How many of those cases are still open? A message that says 'your case is still on our books — are you ready to move forward?' reactivates a meaningful percentage of them before year-end."

### 8. Farmers Branch Auto Repair / Inwood Road Service
**What they do:** Independent auto repair shop in Farmers Branch — likely serving a loyal customer base that has been coming to the same shop for 10–20 years, alongside new customers from the area's commercial density.

**Specific observation:** The fall is a natural service window — oil changes, tire rotations, fluid checks before the holiday driving season. A shop that has a customer's mileage from their last visit can calculate who is due right now and send a message. Most shops wait for the customer to come in on their own schedule. The ones actively reaching out fill the schedule through September and October.

**Hook:** "You have every customer's last visit and the mileage from that appointment. The customers who are due for service right now — how are they finding that out? A text that says 'You're probably due for an oil change and tire rotation — click here to pick a time' books appointments before they forget or go somewhere that reminded them first."

### 9. Carrollton / Addison Optometry — Vision Care Associates or similar
**What they do:** Independent optometry, likely seeing a mix of routine annual exams, contact lens fittings, and eyeglass dispensing. Strong pediatric volume in Carrollton given school density.

**Specific observation:** Annual exam recalls are a consistent weak spot for independent optometrists. The patients who had an exam 13 months ago are technically overdue but may not have received a message worth acting on. A specific outreach — "Your annual eye exam was a year ago. September and October are less busy than August — here are open times" — converts better than a generic recall card. Contact lens wearers are especially responsive since they have a tangible supply need that drives the behavior.

**Hook:** "Your patients who were last in 12–14 months ago — how are they hearing from you? If it's a postcard, you're probably getting 6–8% call-in rate. A text with a direct scheduling link gets closer to 18–22%. That's a significant difference in how full your October looks."

### 10. Addison Fitness / Boutique Studio (Addison)
**What they do:** Boutique fitness studio — HIIT, yoga, cycling, Pilates — in an affluent zip code. High member willingness-to-pay, high dropout rate if the connection to the studio feels impersonal.

**Specific observation:** September is back-to-routine month for fitness. Lapsed members from summer who went on vacation, reduced their class frequency, or drifted away are the highest-conversion re-engagement target of the year. A "September Restart" message to anyone who hasn't attended a class in 45+ days — with a direct link to sign up for a class and maybe a one-class-free offer — wins back a meaningful percentage before they decide to join a different studio or not come back at all.

**Hook:** "Your members who haven't checked in since June or July — how many names is that? September is the highest re-engagement window of the year. A message that says 'we missed you this summer — here's a class on us to come back' usually gets 15–20% response. Do you have a system that sends that message, or does it only happen if someone remembers to do it?"
