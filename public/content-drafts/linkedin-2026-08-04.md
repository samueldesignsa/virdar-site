Most barbershop owners can tell you exactly who their regulars are.

They know the name. The fade. The face. Some of them have cut the same guy for six years.

They have zero of those customers' phone numbers.

When school starts August 19, every parent in Lewisville ISD will be getting their kid a haircut. The shops that send a single text — "school starts the 19th, we have morning slots this week for school haircuts" — fill Monday and Tuesday before the Thursday walk-in rush hits.

The shops that don't have a contact list wait for the surge and hope it comes early enough.

This is the walk-in economy problem. You do the work. You build the relationship. You earn the loyalty. But the transaction is cash, no name collected, and when the customer walks out the door, they're gone. You can't reach them for back-to-school. You can't reach them for their birthday. You can't reach them when they're overdue.

Building that list takes two to three months of a simple text-to-join prompt at the register.

Not having it costs you every seasonal moment for the rest of the year.
