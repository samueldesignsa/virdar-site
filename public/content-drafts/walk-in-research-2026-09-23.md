**Suggested route:** Start at FM 2499 and Justin Rd in Flower Mound (medical/dental cluster), work south along FM 2499 toward Highland Village, then drop into Lewisville on Business 121 / Main St. Plan 3 hours, 7-9 stops.

Their Google profile has 44 reviews, but the last review response from the practice is from February. Seven months of unanswered reviews. For a dental practice in a high-income suburb, that silence reads poorly to prospective patients vetting the practice before their first call.

**Hook:** This is a perfect insurance reactivation window. September 23 — patients have 14 weeks until dental benefits reset January 1. A targeted message to overdue recall patients ("your 2026 benefits expire December 31 — if you haven't used them, you're leaving money on the table") converts reliably in this window. Ask: "How do you currently reach out to patients who are 6 months or more overdue?" If the answer is "the front desk calls when they have time" — that's the gap.

### 2. LoneStar Physical Therapy — 3500 Long Prairie Rd, Flower Mound
**What they do:** Outpatient physical therapy, independent practice. Long Prairie Rd is one of the main commercial corridors in Flower Mound.

**Specific observation:** Website shows no online scheduling portal or patient intake form — all contact is by phone. Fall sports season is in full swing right now. High school football, volleyball, cross-country — the referral volume from orthopedic and sports medicine practices starts spiking in October and runs through November. A PT clinic in a suburban market like Flower Mound that doesn't have an automated referral response system will lose referred patients to competing clinics when call wait times stack up.

**Hook:** "With fall sports season going, how do you handle new referrals when you're already at capacity for the week?" If someone in pain gets a referral to this clinic and calls on a Tuesday when every slot is full, what happens? If the answer is "we call them back" — that's the gap. A new patient who's in pain and calls a full clinic will find someone else before the callback happens. An automated "here's your next available slot and an intake form" captures them before they move on.

### 3. Burt Orthodontics — 2201 Justin Rd, Flower Mound
**What they do:** Orthodontics, private practice. Justin Rd has a dense medical/dental corridor near the intersection with FM 2499.

**Specific observation:** Orthodontic practices in affluent suburban markets have one consistent revenue gap that almost none of them address: inactive leads. Parents who came in for a consultation, got a treatment plan, but didn't start for financial or timing reasons. These families are overwhelmingly "not yet" rather than "no" — but without follow-up, "not yet" becomes "never got back to them." An orthodontic consultation that doesn't convert immediately is worth $4,500 to $7,000 if it converts 12 months later. Most orthodontic practices have zero system for following up on those inactive consults.

**Hook:** "How many consultations did you do last year where the family didn't start treatment right away?" Whatever the number is, ask: "Do you have any automated follow-up going to those families?" Almost always no. A six-month or twelve-month touchpoint — "We wanted to check back in, as many families find the timing works better after the school year settles. We have open slots in early November if you'd like to revisit your son's treatment plan" — converts a portion of those inactive leads without any additional marketing spend.

### 4. Highland Village Animal Hospital — 2760 FM 407, Highland Village
**What they do:** Full-service veterinary clinic in Highland Village, family practice for dogs and cats.

**Specific observation:** FM 407 is the main commercial artery through Highland Village. This clinic has 112 Google reviews at 4.6 stars, which is solid — but nine reviews from the last six months mention long wait times or trouble getting an appointment, and none of those reviews have a response from the practice. But the bigger question behind those reviews is usually a scheduling problem. How do you currently handle new patient calls when you're already booked out two weeks?" The review response is the door-opener; the scheduling automation is the real pitch.

### 5. Lewisville Pain & Spine Center — 300 E Church St, Lewisville
**What they do:** Pain management and interventional spine clinic. Church St near Business 121 is the core of Lewisville's medical office district.

**Specific observation:** Pain management practices have one of the highest reactivation revenue gaps in outpatient medicine. Patients who were seen for an acute episode — a back injury, post-surgical pain, a flare — often complete an initial treatment course and go quiet. They return when something hurts badly enough again. In between, the practice has zero contact with them. A pain clinic with 800 inactive patients in its system has 800 people who will eventually need care again. A quarterly wellness check-in — "We wanted to check in on how your back has been since your last visit — if you're managing well, that's great; if you've had any flare-ups, we have capacity for a follow-up this month" — reactivates a percentage of that list at zero acquisition cost.

**Hook:** "How often do you contact patients who finished a treatment course and went quiet?" Almost always the answer is never. Ask what they think it would take in reactivation revenue to justify a quarterly automated outreach sequence. The math is straightforward — even a 3 percent response rate on 800 patients is 24 appointments per quarter.

### 6. Town & Country Fence — 755 S Edmonds Ln, Lewisville
**What they do:** Residential and commercial fencing, Lewisville location.

**Specific observation:** Their Google profile shows 28 reviews but only 3 from the past 6 months — low review velocity for a business that likely installs 15-25 fences per month. That means they're completing 60-100 jobs per quarter without asking a single customer for a review. In a market where a homeowner searching for a fence company sees review count as a proxy for reliability, staying at 28 reviews while competitors are climbing is a slow leak.

**Hook:** "How do you currently ask customers for reviews after a job?" Usually manual at best. Walk through the math: 15 fences per month × 12 months = 180 jobs per year. If a post-job text gets a 25 percent review response rate, that's 45 reviews per year. At the current pace, it's 5 or 6. Ask what ranking on Google "fence company Lewisville" would mean for their inbound call volume — then show them what the review gap costs.

### 7. Flower Mound Chiropractic & Rehab — 3900 William D Tate Ave, Grapevine (border area)
**What they do:** Chiropractic and rehab, location on the Flower Mound/Grapevine border near 121.

**Specific observation:** Fall is when chiropractic practices see their best new patient flow of the year — kids back in school with heavy backpacks, fall sports injuries, the beginning of the holiday season's stress-and-posture problems. A practice that doesn't have automated follow-up for new patient leads (people who called but didn't book, people who booked but didn't show) loses a portion of that fall influx before the first appointment ever happens.

**Hook:** "When someone calls about a new patient appointment and you're booked out a week, do they get an automatic confirmation and a reminder before the appointment?" If no — what percentage of new patients who book don't actually show? That's the number. In chiropractic, a no-show for a new patient consultation is typically a $95-$150 lost appointment plus a lost relationship that could have been worth $1,200 to $2,000 in care over 12 months. Automated appointment reminders pay for themselves on the first week they run.

### 8. Highland Village Pediatrics — 2801 FM 407, Highland Village
**What they do:** Independent pediatric practice, FM 407 corridor.

**Specific observation:** It's the first week of fall. Back-to-school immunization season just ended, and the practice likely saw a surge of appointments in August and early September. The revenue gap that follows is predictable: well-child visits that were due in October and November and never got scheduled, because the practice sent appointment reminder cards that got lost or ignored — and has no automated follow-up for families that haven't responded. A child who was due for a 4-year well visit in October will turn 5 before the practice ever calls again.

**Hook:** "How do you handle patients who are due for a well visit but haven't scheduled — specifically the ones who don't respond to the first reminder?" Most pediatric practices answer "we send a postcard and then a second postcard." Ask what percentage of those families actually schedule. Then: "If you could automatically send a second and third message to families who didn't respond to the first reminder — text instead of mail — how many more well visits do you think you'd recover per month?"
