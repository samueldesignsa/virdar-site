## One Insight

Today's tattoo studio post surfaced something that applies across every industry Beverly visits: **the speed gap is a structural problem, not a personal one.**

Artists aren't slow to respond because they don't care about bookings. They're slow because they physically cannot respond during business hours — they're in a session with a client. The same is true for a dentist who can't answer the phone between appointments, a physical therapist who doesn't check the inquiry form until after the last patient, a chiropractor who's booked solid until 6pm.

The prospect doesn't know this. The prospect knows: I sent an inquiry and got a response four hours later, and someone else responded in ten minutes. The prospect goes with whoever responded.

This is the universal pitch. Every industry Beverly walks into today faces some version of this: the hours when the business is busiest are the same hours when new patient inquiries are most likely to arrive, and the person who would normally respond is occupied with the patient or client already in front of them. The businesses that solve this — with an automated acknowledgment, a scheduling link, a next-step that doesn't require the owner to stop what they're doing — convert inquiries that their competitors lose.

The question Beverly can use anywhere today: "When someone submits an inquiry form on your website at 2pm on a Tuesday, what happens? Walk me through it." Almost no business owner has a good answer. That pause is the conversation.
