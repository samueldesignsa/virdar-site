A dental office in Flower Mound has seen this every October for eight years.

Patients call in September wanting to get a cleaning before their benefits reset in January. The front desk is slammed. They tell people the next available slot is November. Some book it. A lot of them say "I'll call back" and never do.

By the time February rolls around, those patients have lapsed. Nobody followed up. Nobody reactivated them in January when the new benefit year started. They're just gone — or they're at a different dentist.

I talked to the owner about it. He said his team knows the problem. They just don't have time to call through a list of 200 people who haven't been in for 18 months.

That's the gap. Not strategy. Not desire. Just bandwidth.

An automated recall sequence handles this without staff time. Texts go out in late September and October to every patient with an unscheduled recall. Then a January wave to patients who didn't schedule in the fall. The front desk only handles people who respond.

Two months of automated outreach. Patients who would have lapsed, rebooked. No extra staff hours.

The insurance reset window is the single most predictable revenue moment in dentistry. Most offices don't work it. They could.
