# Walk-In Research — Thursday, October 8, 2026
## Territory: Keller / North Richland Hills

Keller and North Richland Hills (NRH) sit in northeastern Tarrant County — between Fort Worth and the Mid-Cities corridor. This is a different market character from the high-income North Dallas suburbs like Southlake or Southlake-adjacent Colleyville, and very different from the Ellis County markets covered this week. Keller/NRH is solidly middle-income: dual-income families, working professionals, strong youth sports culture, high homeownership rates. The commercial corridors are denser than Ellis County and the businesses are bigger — multi-provider practices, 3–5 truck HVAC companies, roofing contractors doing real volume. But vendor saturation is meaningfully lower than Frisco, McKinney, or Allen. These are businesses that have grown with the residential base and are behind on their systems, but they're not so small that they can't buy.

The key corridors: Precinct Line Road (runs east-west through NRH into Hurst), Davis Blvd (north-south, Keller's main commercial spine), Rufe Snow Drive (NRH), and Keller Pkwy (SH 1709). Route is compact — most of these businesses are within 5–7 miles of each other. A full morning covers all of them.

Today's specific hooks: year-end dental and vision benefits, FSA/HSA deadlines, fall HVAC inquiry surge, summer roofing estimate backlog, fall sports injury volume for chiropractic and PT.

## 1. Keller Family Dental
**Address:** 1341 Keller Pkwy (SH 1709), Keller, TX 76248
**What they do:** General and family dentistry — cleanings, restorations, crowns, and cosmetic work. Serves Keller and Southlake residential families.
**What I noticed:** October is the live window for year-end dental benefits recall. Delta Dental, Cigna, and Aetna plans all reset January 1. Patients who haven't used their cleaning or treatment benefit this year are in the decision window right now — motivated by the "use it before you lose it" deadline. A Keller family practice has a middle-to-upper-income residential base where insurance utilization is high and patients will schedule when reminded. Most practices this size rely on patients calling in to schedule rather than sending proactive recall.
**Best hook:** "October is the best recall window dental practices see all year. Your patients have Delta Dental, Cigna, and Aetna benefits expiring December 31 — they're actually motivated to schedule right now. Are you reaching the patients who haven't been in since spring, or waiting for them to call?"

## 2. NRH Heating & Cooling
**Address:** 7401 Precinct Line Rd, North Richland Hills, TX 76182
**What they do:** Residential HVAC — repairs, replacements, tune-ups, and maintenance agreements. Serves NRH, Hurst, Euless, and Colleyville residential customers.
**What I noticed:** NRH's housing stock is heavily 1990s–2010s construction — a big percentage of homes are now 15–30 years old and in the HVAC replacement window. October is when homeowners turn the heat on for the first time and discover something's wrong. Those replacement inquiries come in the evening, after business hours. A company that responds that night gets the job. A company that calls back the next morning is often calling a customer who already booked someone else. In NRH, a system replacement runs $9,000–$14,000.
**Best hook:** "October is when replacement inquiries spike in this market — homeowners turning on heat for the first time and finding something wrong. If someone submits your form at 7pm on a cold night, does anything go back to them that night? In a first-response market like HVAC, whoever replies first usually gets the call."

## 3. Keller Physical Therapy & Sports Rehab
**Address:** 901 Keller Pkwy, Suite 100, Keller, TX 76248
**What they do:** Outpatient physical therapy — sports injuries, post-surgical rehab, orthopedic conditions. Keller's youth sports culture (baseball, soccer, football, volleyball) drives injury volume through fall sports seasons.
**What I noticed:** Two things are live simultaneously in October: FSA balances expiring December 31 (PT is one of the most commonly used FSA categories), and fall sports injuries from Keller ISD and nearby programs peaking now. Patients who came in once for an eval or started treatment and dropped off have a standing order and FSA money they're about to lose. A proactive outreach to lapsed patients — "you have a standing order and benefits expiring, let's get scheduled before December" — converts at high rates because the patient already knows they need the care.
**Best hook:** "FSA money expires December 31. You have patients with a standing order who've been meaning to come back — they have pre-tax money they're about to lose and they know it. Are you reaching those patients right now, or is it more organic scheduling?"

## 5. Rufe Snow Eye Care
**Address:** 8540 Rufe Snow Dr, North Richland Hills, TX 76180
**What they do:** Optometry — comprehensive exams, eyewear, contact lenses. Serves NRH and surrounding mid-cities residential population.
**What I noticed:** VSP and EyeMed benefits reset January 1. October is when patients who haven't used their annual exam benefit realize they're about to lose it. ## 6. North Tarrant Roofing
**Address:** 4821 Davis Blvd, North Richland Hills, TX 76180
**What they do:** Residential and light commercial roofing. Serves Tarrant County residential market — NRH, Keller, Hurst, Colleyville, and surrounding areas.
**What I noticed:** North Tarrant County had hail activity earlier this year. A roofing company on Davis Blvd gave estimates through spring and summer that haven't all closed. October is the window for homeowners who didn't act during the summer rush but are now motivated before winter. Most roofing contractors let those estimates sit. A single automated follow-up to every open summer estimate would close a meaningful number of jobs — and in NRH, a full replacement runs $12,000–$18,000 per job.
**Best hook:** "Quick question — how many open estimates from spring or summer haven't closed yet? October is when those homeowners actually decide before winter. One follow-up message to that whole list right now would close jobs your competitors don't know are still available."

Don't lead with tech. Lead with: "You have a patient panel that is about to lose money, and there's a specific window to reach them before that happens."
