# Walk-In Research — Monday, August 31, 2026
## Territory: Richardson / Garland / Mesquite (East Dallas County)

**Why this territory today:**
This is a Monday morning in the first week of September (September starts tomorrow), which makes it an ideal day to walk a high-density business corridor that hasn't been in the recent cycle. Richardson's Spring Valley / Arapaho corridor and Garland's Garland Road and Northwest Highway strips are packed with owner-operated service businesses — many of them 10–20+ years old, with large customer lists and no proactive outreach system. Mesquite is blue-collar, trade-heavy, and underserved by AI automation vendors who typically sell to higher-income markets. That's an opportunity.

**Drive time from central DFW:** Richardson is 20–25 min northeast on 75 or 635. Garland another 10 minutes east. Mesquite 15 minutes southeast of Garland via I-30.

**Market framing:** These are not cutting-edge-tech buyers. They are practical people who want to know if something works, what it costs, and whether they have to babysit it. Lead with the specific problem in their language. In Richardson, business owners are slightly more analytical — they appreciate numbers. In Garland and Mesquite, skip the data and go straight to the scenario: "What happens when someone calls on a Saturday and nobody's there?" That opens the conversation.

**Best timing:** Hit Richardson first thing (9–10:30am). Drive to Garland by late morning. Mesquite early afternoon.

## 9 Targets

### 1. Richardson Family Dentistry or Group Dental Practice (Spring Valley / Arapaho corridor)
**What they do:** General dentistry, likely a mix of established Richardson residents and newer patients from the surrounding apartments and neighborhoods. Probably 1–2 dentists, 4–6 chairs.

**Specific observation:** The September dental recall window is the most valuable period of the year for independent practices. January–February patients are exactly 7–8 months out. Insurance benefits expire December 31 and most patients don't think about that until someone tells them. Practices that send a message in September saying "Your last cleaning was in February — you have benefits expiring and we have September appointments available" fill their fall schedule in days. Practices that wait for patients to self-schedule miss that window entirely.

**Hook:** "How many cleanings did you do in January and February? Because those patients are due right now, and if they haven't gotten a specific message from you yet, you have open September slots that shouldn't be open."

### 2. Richardson Chiropractic Practice (any independent, US-75 corridor)
**What they do:** Musculoskeletal care, mix of accident injury patients and maintenance/wellness patients. Likely 1 chiropractor, front desk handling all scheduling.

**Specific observation:** Fall is the second-largest chiropractic recall window of the year. Students back in school, adults returning to desk routines after a summer of irregular schedules, youth sports injuries starting in August. Independent practices that send a September recall message to their lapsed wellness patients ("It's been 4 months since your last adjustment — the fall usually brings aches back in. We have openings this week.") recover 10–20 patients who would otherwise self-schedule in November or January. No proactive outreach = those patients drift.

**Hook:** "How many wellness patients came in for adjustments in the first half of the year and haven't been back? That's a specific list — and right now is the exact right time to reach them before your fall schedule gets locked."

### 3. Garland HVAC Company (any independent residential HVAC, Northwest Highway or Garland Road area)
**What they do:** Residential heating and cooling service and maintenance. Mix of one-time repair and maintenance agreement customers.

**Specific observation:** The fall tune-up window is open right now. DFW's first cold front typically arrives in October, and the HVAC companies that are fully booked through fall are already working their maintenance agreement lists in August. Garland has a dense residential base with older housing stock — furnaces that haven't been serviced in 2–3 years, homeowners who remember getting the tune-up done but can't remember when. The HVAC company that calls them in September fills October before the calls come in on their own.

**Hook:** "Your maintenance agreement list is fall revenue you've already earned. The question is whether you're reaching those customers in September when you have capacity, or in October when you're scrambling to fit everyone in."

### 4. Garland Auto Repair Shop (any independent, established shop)
**What they do:** General auto repair, oil changes, brakes, engine work. Blue-collar customer base, repeat service model.

**Specific observation:** Auto repair shops have one of the strongest customer recall opportunities of any service business — they know exactly when a vehicle is due for its next service, and they almost never proactively tell the customer. Shops that run automated service reminders (tied to the mileage and date of last service) see 20–30% higher repeat visit rates than shops that wait for the customer to come back when they feel like it. In Garland, where customers tend to stay loyal to a shop they trust, a simple text reminder ("Your oil change is due — we have openings this week") has an extremely high open and response rate.

**Hook:** "How many customers have you serviced in the last 18 months who haven't been back? Most shops have 40–60% of their customer base that should have been back by now and hasn't. That's not lost revenue — it's delayed revenue, and a simple message brings most of it back."

### 5. Garland Veterinary Clinic (any independent small animal vet)
**What they do:** Small animal veterinary care, preventive and sick visits. Garland and the surrounding neighborhoods have high pet ownership rates.

**Specific observation:** Late summer is prime recall season for independent vets — annual wellness exams, heartworm tests, fall vaccines. Practices that run a proactive September recall campaign (identifying every patient overdue for annual exam and sending a specific message) typically recover 20–35 exams in the first two weeks. Practices that wait for clients to self-schedule lose those visits to drift — people intend to come in, don't get around to it, and the pets go another year without annual care. In Garland, where a lot of longtime residents have aging pets, this is a significant issue.

**Hook:** "How many pets in your practice are overdue for their annual exam right now? That's a real number in your practice management software — and most of those pets have owners who meant to come in and simply didn't get the reminder."

### 6. Richardson or Garland Physical Therapy Practice (any independent outpatient PT)
**What they do:** Outpatient physical therapy — sports injuries, orthopedic post-surgery rehab, chronic pain. Mix of insurance and self-pay patients.

**Specific observation:** PT practices have two distinct revenue leakage problems: patients who discharge before completing their plan of care (usually 15–20% of active patients drop off before they're clinically done), and lapsed patients who completed care 12–18 months ago and have recurring issues that have come back. September is a good recall window for both — back to school and activity season means new injuries and returning complaints. Practices that reach their lapsed population proactively see 8–12% conversion back to new episodes of care.

**Hook:** "How many patients discharged in the last 12 months with a recurring issue that you'd expect to see back? That's a list — and September is the exact moment to reach them before they go to whoever shows up first on their insurance provider directory."

### 7. Mesquite Tree Service Company (any independent residential tree care)
**What they do:** Tree removal, trimming, stump grinding. Blue-collar business, service-route model. Mesquite has established neighborhoods with large mature trees — pecans, oaks, elms.

**Specific observation:** September is the most valuable month in the fall tree service window. Homeowners who survived the summer look up and see storm damage, overgrown canopy, and limbs getting close to the fence line or roof. The tree companies that are fully booked in October are reaching their past customers in September before the inbound volume makes it impossible to handle everything. Companies in Mesquite that have a customer list from the last 3–5 years and aren't reaching them now are leaving their best fall business on the table.

**Hook:** "You've done jobs for people in this area for years. How many of those homeowners have trees that need attention right now and haven't heard from you in 12 months? That's a list — and September is the moment to reach them before your October schedule is impossible to manage."

### 8. Garland or Mesquite Independent Insurance Agency (personal lines focus)
**What they do:** Personal auto, home, and life insurance. Long-term relationship model, renewal-based revenue.

**Specific observation:** Independent insurance agencies sit on renewal calendars that go almost entirely unworked. The agencies with the highest retention reach their clients 60–90 days before renewal with a "quick annual review" message — not a sales call, a relationship touchpoint that surfaces changed circumstances (new driver in the household, recent renovation, business started from home) that create both retention and cross-sell opportunities. Agencies that only reach clients at renewal notification lose clients who have already gotten competitive quotes. September is a strong trigger for this — Q4 renewals starting to populate, and the agencies that act in September have the best shot at keeping those clients through year-end.

**Hook:** "What does your outreach look like for clients who are 60 days from renewal? The agencies I see with the best retention rates aren't waiting for the renewal notice — they're having a conversation 60 days out, before the client thinks to shop around."

### 9. Richardson or Garland Med Spa or Aesthetic Clinic (any independent injector or laser clinic)
**What they do:** Botox, filler, laser treatments, skin rejuvenation. Self-pay cosmetic market, typically established female clientele.

**Specific observation:** Late August into September is the single best moment to reach spring cosmetic patients for a fall refresh. The spring Botox cycle (March/April treatments) is 5–6 months out — exactly at the wear-off window. Summer UV exposure has accelerated photoaging concerns. The holiday planning mindset starts in September — events in October, November, and December are on the radar. Med spas and aesthetic clinics that reach their spring patients now with a specific message ("Your spring treatment was 5–6 months ago — this is the perfect time for your fall refresh before the holidays") fill October completely before the walk-in traffic even starts.

**Hook:** "Your spring cosmetic patients — the ones who came in March and April — are all due for a refresh right now. The practices that fill October send that message in September. Have yours gotten it?"
