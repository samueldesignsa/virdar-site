## One Insight

**Frisco is a high-reward walk-in territory — but the pitch needs to shift.**

Most of the businesses you'll walk into today are not struggling. The dentist on Preston Road is busy. The med spa on Legacy Drive has a waitlist for injections. The boutique gym in McKinney is half-full. These owners aren't desperate for revenue — they're managing growth.

That changes the approach. Don't open with pain. Open with opportunity: "You're already building a strong customer base here — the businesses I work with that are at this stage have one thing in common, which is a customer list that isn't being worked between visits. That's where the next revenue layer comes from."

The Frisco/McKinney owner who's doing well is a better buyer for a system than the struggling owner who's worried about next month's rent. They have capital, they're thinking about scale, and they're already investing in marketing. The pitch is about building on what's working, not fixing what's broken.

That framing is also lower friction. You're not telling them their business has a problem — you're showing them where the next growth comes from. That conversation is easier to have, easier to book a follow-up on, and more likely to close.
