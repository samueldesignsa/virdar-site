# Walk-In Research — Wednesday, October 21, 2026
## Territory: Southlake / Colleyville / Keller

Southlake is consistently ranked one of the wealthiest cities in Texas — median household income above $180,000, a housing stock built almost entirely between 1995 and 2015, and a concentration of dual-income professional families that is unmatched in the DFW metro outside of Westlake and Highland Park. The Colleyville and Keller corridors adjacent to it carry much of the same demographic profile at a slightly lower price point. The Town Square / East Southlake Boulevard corridor is the commercial spine: a planned, walkable district with medical offices, specialty retail, and service businesses targeting the professional household.

This market has not been covered in recent walk-in research. It should be a priority.

The October hooks are all live here — and they hit harder than in most DFW territories because of the demographics:

- **FSA/HSA**: Southlake and Colleyville households are deeply benefits-aware. Dual incomes at financial services, corporate, and healthcare employers mean these patients have loaded FSA and HSA accounts — often the maximum. The $2,750 FSA use-it-or-lose-it message is not a discovery here; it's a trigger. These patients have been meaning to schedule and they will act when someone gives them a specific reason.
- **Year-end deductible**: High-deductible health plans are common in this demographic, and they've often hit or nearly hit the deductible by Q4. The "your deductible resets January 1" message lands hard on a patient who has already paid $3,000 toward a $6,000 family deductible and still has six weeks of subsidized care available.
- **Holiday prep**: This demographic is active socially through December — fundraisers, corporate events, school events, holiday entertaining. Medical aesthetics, dental cosmetic work, and photography sessions are in high demand from October through Thanksgiving. The practices that reach their patients first with a "book now for December" message fill the calendar from their own list.

**Wednesday note:** Southlake-area medical practices are typically fully staffed mid-week. Arrive between 9am and 10am for practice managers. The Town Square corridor is accessible from State Highway 114 and East Southlake Boulevard. Keller and Colleyville businesses along Rufe Snow Drive and Precinct Line Road are 10–20 minutes from Southlake Town Square and make natural afternoon stops. Total driving for a full Southlake–Colleyville–Keller day is under 30 minutes.

## 1. Independent Dental Practice — Southlake Town Square or East Southlake Blvd
**Address:** Look for owner-operated general or cosmetic dental practices in or around Southlake Town Square (1 S. Village Ct, Southlake TX 76092) or along East Southlake Boulevard between Carroll Avenue and White Chapel Boulevard, Southlake TX 76092. The Southlake corridor has a high concentration of independent dental practices serving one of Texas's wealthiest residential markets.
**What they do:** Full-service general and cosmetic dentistry — cleanings, restorations, Invisalign, whitening, veneers, implants — for a patient base with access to premium employer dental plans and strong disposable income for elective work. Annual dental benefit maximums of $2,500–$5,000 are common in this demographic through employer plans at financial services, energy, and healthcare companies.
**What I noticed:** The Southlake dental patient is benefit-aware and aesthetically motivated. October is the last window to reach patients with open treatment plans or unused benefit before they make holiday social commitments. The practices that send a specific, personal message to open-estimate patients in October — "your benefit expires December 31, and we have openings in November for the work you discussed" — convert a list that would otherwise age out. Most practices send a generic benefits reminder in November. The one that sends a personalized October message wins the November calendar.
**Best hook:** "Your patients here have strong plans — and a lot of them have been sitting on an open treatment estimate or an Invisalign consult they haven't scheduled. October is the window: year-end benefit expires December 31, and the patients who want to look good at a December event are booking their cosmetic work right now. Are you reaching your open-estimate patients with anything specific? The practices that do in October fill November — the ones that send a generic November reminder compete with everyone else for last-minute dates."

## 2. Med Spa / Aesthetic Clinic — Southlake or Colleyville
**Address:** Look for owner-operated medical spas or aesthetic clinics in Southlake Town Square or on East Southlake Boulevard, Southlake TX 76092, or along Colleyville Boulevard between Hall-Johnson Road and Glade Road, Colleyville TX 76034. This demographic is a prime med spa market — affluent, aesthetically active, and repeat-visit.
**What they do:** Medical aesthetics — Botox, filler, laser treatments, body contouring, chemical peels, medical-grade facials — for the affluent Southlake and Colleyville patient. The client base runs $3,000–$8,000/year at a single provider and is not price-sensitive. Retention is built on relationship and results, not price.
**What I noticed:** October is the best single month for a Southlake-area med spa to activate its list, for three reasons that converge simultaneously: (1) FSA use-it-or-lose-it — $2,750 max, $660 carryover, so a patient with $1,200 in their FSA has $540 to spend before December 31 or lose it; (2) holiday prep — Botox and filler need 2–4 weeks to fully settle, meaning a patient who wants to look sharp for a December event needs to book in October; (3) gift cards — Southlake clients are heavy gift card buyers for the holidays, and a $250 med spa gift card is a go-to for someone shopping for the woman who has everything. A practice with 400 active patients doing no October outreach is sitting on $25,000–$50,000 in FSA/holiday revenue it will never recover.
**Best hook:** "Your patients have FSA money expiring December 31 — and patients who want Botox or filler for a December event need to book this month, not November. And your clients here are the demographic that gives med spa gift cards for the holidays. That's three specific reasons to send a message to your list right now. Are you reaching your active patients with any of this, or waiting until November when the calendar is already half-booked?"

## 3. Pediatric Dentistry or Orthodontics — Southlake / Keller
**Address:** Look for pediatric dental or orthodontic practices near Carroll Avenue and East Southlake Boulevard in Southlake TX 76092, or along Keller Parkway (FM 1709) between Davis Boulevard and Bear Creek Parkway in Keller TX 76248. The Southlake/Keller demographic is dense with families — K–12 school enrollment is at capacity in both districts, and orthodontics and pediatric dental are among the highest-volume outpatient services in these zip codes.
**What they do:** Pediatric dentistry (cleanings, sealants, fluoride, fillings) and orthodontics (traditional braces, Invisalign Teen, growth-phase treatment) for the school-age population. Insurance plans through Southlake and Keller-area employers are typically strong — Delta Dental PPO and Cigna plans with $1,500–$2,000 pediatric maximums are common. Many families also carry ortho riders with lifetime maximums that reset at a new plan year.
**What I noticed:** Pediatric dental and orthodontic practices in Southlake and Keller have a specific October opportunity: families that enrolled children in treatment earlier this year have been paying down their annual maximum, and the benefit resets January 1. A family with a $1,800 dental benefit that has already used $1,400 has $400 available before year-end that they will lose if they don't schedule. More importantly, families that got an ortho consultation in spring and didn't start are reconsidering now — parents know the plan year resets and are looking for a reason to act before they lose their start-of-year advantage. The practice that sends an October reminder to "families with an open ortho consult from earlier this year" converts a list that ages out otherwise.
**Best hook:** "Families in Southlake and Keller who had orthodontic consults earlier this year and didn't start are reconsidering now — the plan year resets January 1 and they know it. The practices that reach those open consults in October with a specific 'here's what starting this year means for your benefit year' message convert families who were already interested but needed a push. Are you doing any outreach to your open consult list, or waiting for them to call back on their own?"

## 4. Independent Optometry — Southlake / Colleyville
**Address:** Look for owner-operated optometry practices near Southlake Town Square, East Southlake Boulevard, or on Glade Road in Colleyville TX 76034. The Colleyville/Southlake corridor has a strong independent optometry market serving a patient base with premium VSP and EyeMed coverage.
**What they do:** Comprehensive eye exams, premium frame selection, and specialty contact lens fitting. The Southlake and Colleyville patient pays above their VSP/EyeMed allowance for premium European frames and chooses independent practices for the selection and relationship — LensCrafters and Warby Parker don't compete on the same field in this market.
**What I noticed:** The year-end optometry window in Southlake/Colleyville is the strongest in DFW outside of Highland Park. VSP and EyeMed allowances expire December 31 for most employer plan years ($200–$250 frame credit, $150–$200 exam benefit), and the FSA layer sits on top for patients who haven't used their full balance. An independent optometrist in Southlake with a panel of 1,200 active patients who hasn't sent an October benefits reminder is leaving $40,000–$80,000 in uncaptured care on the table — the exact revenue that LensCrafters captures with its "benefits expiring" email blast and the OD is better positioned to convert.
**Best hook:** "Your patients here have strong VSP and EyeMed coverage, and a lot of them also have FSA balances — and both reset or expire by December 31. The patients who haven't had their annual exam yet are making decisions about where to go right now. Are you reaching them proactively with a specific message about benefits expiring? Most independent ODs wait for inbound and lose the November window to whatever email the benefit manager sends the patient directly."

Southlake-area PT practices see a mix of surgical rehabilitation, sports medicine, and elective chronic pain management for the active professional demographic.
**What they do:** Orthopedic PT or chiropractic care for the active professional and recreational athlete demographic. Southlake patients play competitive adult sports (pickleball, soccer, golf, tennis), have active school-age kids with sports injuries, and work desk jobs that produce chronic neck and back issues. High deductibles from premium employer plans are common — $3,000–$6,000 family deductibles with separate out-of-pocket maximums.
**What I noticed:** The year-end deductible hook is the single strongest argument in physical therapy and chiropractic right now, and the Southlake patient has real deductibles and real math. A family that hit $3,200 of a $4,000 deductible in Q1–Q3 has $800 remaining before their care is nearly free — and January 1 resets that to zero. The PT or chiropractor that sends a specific October message to patients who completed partial plans earlier in the year — "your deductible is close to met for the year, and your remaining sessions before January 1 will cost you significantly less than January sessions" — converts a list that otherwise drifts into the new year.
**Best hook:** "Your patients who came in earlier this year and stopped short of finishing their plan still have deductible paid toward their annual maximum — and it resets January 1. That math is a real argument for scheduling now, not in January when their out-of-pocket resets. Are you reaching those patients in October with anything specific about the year-end cost difference? The practices that do fill their November schedule from their own inactive list."
