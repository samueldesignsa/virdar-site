# LinkedIn Post — Friday, October 16, 2026

**Topic:** The real cost of "we'll think about it"
**Target length:** ~165 words
**Post time:** 8–9am CT

---

A contractor I talked to recently had 22 open estimates — jobs he'd visited, measured, and priced in the last 90 days.

He'd followed up on 3 of them.

The other 19? "They'll call when they're ready," he said. "If they wanted it, they'd reach out."

Here's the math: his average job is $4,200. If 4 of those 19 estimates convert — conservative — that's $16,800 sitting uncollected in a spreadsheet nobody's working.

The "they'll call if they want it" logic costs service businesses more than they realize. The buyer who said "we'll think about it" usually isn't buying from a competitor. They're busy, sitting on the decision, and the first business that sends one follow-up message wins it.

Not a pitch. Just a message. "Hey — following up on the estimate I sent. Happy to answer any questions before you decide."

Most business owners don't have a system to send that message reliably. So it doesn't go. The estimate expires. Next quarter starts the same way.

How many open estimates are sitting in your queue right now?
