# Walk-In Research — Thursday, August 27, 2026
## Territory: Grapevine / Colleyville / Southlake (Northeast Tarrant County)

**Why this territory today:**
Northeast Tarrant County — three distinct market tiers in one corridor. Grapevine has a historic Main Street with owner-operated retail and service businesses, many of them 15–25 years established. Colleyville is affluent residential with a dense strip of long-tenured service businesses (dental, medical, HVAC, auto) serving homeowners with household incomes well above the DFW average. Southlake is the highest-income market in the metroplex — boutique medical, high-end salons, medspa, dental groups, and specialty services with high ticket sizes and sophisticated owners who will respond better to a specific ROI number than to a pitch.

This territory has not been in the recent walk-in cycle. It offers a different buyer profile from the last two weeks' markets (blue-collar South DFW, suburban North Collin). In Southlake and Colleyville, price sensitivity is low and ROI clarity is high — "this pays back in 6 weeks" lands better than "this is affordable." In Grapevine, lead with story and specificity over dollar volume.

**Drive time from central DFW:** 25–35 minutes northwest. Park on Main Street Grapevine and walk. Drive to Colleyville/Southlake for the afternoon.

**Market framing:** These owners are not price-resistant. They're credibility-resistant. They've seen every tech vendor in the DFW market come through. The opener that works here is specific: "I was just in this industry — here's what the practice down the road is dealing with." Concrete problem, specific dollar loss, concrete fix. Don't oversell. Don't use "AI" in the first sentence.

## 10 Targets

### 1. Grapevine Chiropractic / Any Independent Chiro on Main Street (Grapevine Main St)
**What they do:** Musculoskeletal care, mostly private pay and insurance mix. Likely 1–2 providers.

**Specific observation:** Grapevine's Main Street chiro practices have strong tourist and local foot traffic in September — fall festivals, Grapefest (September 18–21 this year), and back-to-routine schedules drive walk-in volume. But after-hours inquiries from people who see the sign or the website while walking around often go unanswered until the next morning, and by then 40% have moved on to the next result.

**Hook:** "Grapefest is three weeks out. Your foot traffic will be up — but your online inquiries won't wait until you open Monday morning. What happens to the person who fills out your website form at 9pm on a Saturday?"

### 2. Grapevine Family Dental (Grapevine)
**What they do:** General family dentistry, established patient base of Grapevine residents, mix of longtime and newer patients from Lake Texoma–area home growth.

**Specific observation:** September is the deepest window of the year for unused dental benefits — patients who had their last cleaning in Q1 are now 6–7 months out. The practices that are fully booked in September send specific messages: "Your last cleaning was in February. You have $X remaining on your plan. Here's a link to book." Practices waiting for patients to self-schedule are going to miss September.

**Hook:** "How many of your patients had their last cleaning in January or February and haven't been back? Do you know that number? Because it's probably your highest-revenue opportunity for the next 90 days."

### 3. Colleyville Dermatology / DermTexas or similar (Colleyville Blvd corridor)
**What they do:** Medical dermatology and cosmetic procedures — Botox, filler, skin cancer screenings, acne treatment. Likely 1 MD with 2–3 support staff.

**Specific observation:** Fall is the strongest season for cosmetic dermatology. Summer is over, sun damage is visible, and patients are planning holiday appearances. Practices that send a proactive "summer's over — this is the best time for a skin check and to address summer damage" message in late August/early September capture September and October appointment slots before the Christmas rush. Practices that don't reach out are full by November through word of mouth alone, but they leave 4–6 weeks of gap in September.

**Hook:** "Your patients who did Botox in March are due right now. Your summer skin damage patients are all staring at their mirrors this week. The practices that fill September proactively sent a message last week. Did yours?"

### 4. Colleyville Pediatrics / Kids First Pediatrics or similar (Colleyville)
**What they do:** Pediatric primary care. High volume of well-child visits in August/September tied to school physicals.

**Specific observation:** School physicals are winding down but a significant tail of unscheduled physicals exists in every practice — families who put it off, kids in year-round schools, new students who transferred late. These patients don't get a reminder unless the practice sends one. Pediatricians also routinely see a dip in well-child visits in October–November that can be partially filled by proactively messaging families whose annual physicals are "overdue by month."

**Hook:** "School's been in for two weeks. How many kids in your panel still haven't had their sports physical or well-child visit this year? That's a specific list you can work — they didn't fall through the cracks, they just didn't get a message."

### 5. Southlake MedSpa / Ethos Spa or similar (Southlake Town Square area)
**What they do:** Cosmetic med spa — Botox, filler, laser, body contouring, IV therapy. High-ticket clients, predominantly female 35–55, high-income Southlake/Westlake demographics.

**Specific observation:** Southlake medspas have one of the highest lifetime customer values of any business Beverly walks into. A returning client spending $400–600/visit three times per year is worth $1,200–$1,800/year. The medspa that tracks visit frequency and sends a personalized message when a client is overdue — "It's been 4 months since your last filler appointment, wanted to check in" — retains that client. The one waiting for the client to book on their own loses her to a competitor the next time she sees a coupon on Instagram.

**Hook:** "How many of your clients had an appointment in April or May and haven't been back? That's not a lost client — that's a client who hasn't received a message that made it easy to book. What does your reactivation look like for someone who's been quiet for 90 days?"

### 6. Southlake Auto Repair / Grease Monkey or independent shop (Southlake)
**What they do:** Auto maintenance and repair — oil changes, tires, inspections, brakes. High-volume shop with repeat customers.

**Specific observation:** Auto shops in affluent markets like Southlake have a unique advantage: their customers drive expensive vehicles with high-value maintenance and repair tickets. A BMW or Mercedes owner due for an oil change is worth more than the oil change — if the shop prompts them with a "your vehicle is due for service" message, they often add $200–600 in deferred maintenance while in. Shops without automated mileage-based follow-up are leaving those upsells on the table because the customer doesn't know they're due.

**Hook:** "Your last-visit data has every customer's mileage when they came in. You know who's 2,000 miles overdue right now. Are those customers getting a specific message — 'hey, you're at 47,000 miles, you're due for X' — or are you waiting for them to come in on their own?"

### 7. Grapevine HVAC / Independent Heating and Air Company (Grapevine area)
**What they do:** Residential HVAC service and maintenance. Mix of one-time service and maintenance agreement customers.

**Specific observation:** The first cold front in DFW typically arrives in October. The HVAC companies that are fully booked through October are filling those slots now — reaching their existing maintenance agreement customers with fall tune-up scheduling while the calendar is still open. Companies waiting for September call volume are going to find themselves scrambling. This is the single best timing window of the year for a proactive outreach campaign.

**Hook:** "Your maintenance agreement customers are going to need a fall check before the first cold front. The ones who book proactively are going to fill September. The ones who wait for the phone to ring are going to find October suddenly very full and very stressful. What does your August outreach to your maintenance list look like?"

### 8. Southlake Orthodontics / Smiles of Southlake or similar (Southlake)
**What they do:** Orthodontic treatment — braces, Invisalign. High-ticket per patient ($4,000–6,000 treatment plans), strong referral base from area general dentists.

**Specific observation:** Orthodontic practices have a specific lead problem: people who call for a consultation and don't schedule, or who schedule and don't show. In an affluent market like Southlake, the issue is not usually price — it's friction. The practice that follows up with a specific message ("your consult was three weeks ago — here's what you mentioned being on the fence about") converts a meaningful percentage of those hesitant leads. Most practices do nothing after the consultation goes cold.

**Hook:** "How many consults did you do in July that didn't start treatment? What happened to those leads? In a market like Southlake, the hesitation usually isn't price — it's 'I need to think about it.' The practices that follow up with something specific in week two close those cases. The ones that don't lose them to the next orthodontist they find on Instagram."

### 9. Grapevine Winery / Wine Tasting Room on Main Street (Grapevine)
**What they do:** Wine tasting, club memberships, private events. High-ticket events (wine dinners, private tastings, club membership at $80–150/month).

**Specific observation:** Grapefest (September 18–21) is four weeks out. Wine businesses on Main Street Grapevine have a huge event-season opportunity that most of them prepare for reactively — they wait for people to show up during Grapefest rather than using August to pre-sell their wine club memberships, private event spots, and post-festival experiences to their existing email list.

**Hook:** "Your wine club members and past event attendees are the most likely people to book something during Grapefest. Are you reaching out to them specifically in August — 'here's what we're doing during Grapefest, here's how to get first access' — or are you waiting for the foot traffic to show up and hoping they remember you?"

### 10. Colleyville Physical Therapy / Star Physical Therapy or similar (Colleyville)
**What they do:** Outpatient physical therapy — orthopedic, sports, post-surgical. Mix of insurance and private pay, referral-heavy.

**Specific observation:** Physical therapy practices are heavily dependent on physician referrals, but they often under-invest in nurturing existing patients back. A patient who finished a 12-week course of PT for a knee injury six months ago may have a new issue — or may not have fully addressed the original one. The PT practice that sends a check-in message at the 6-month mark captures a meaningful percentage of those patients before they look for a new provider from scratch.

**Hook:** "How many patients completed a plan of care in the first quarter and haven't been back? Six months out is when secondary complaints usually surface. If you're not reaching out to that group with something specific — 'we've been thinking about your progress, wanted to check in on how you're doing' — you're finding out about their new issue when they book with someone else."

## Today's Standout Stop

**Southlake MedSpa (any independent medspa near Town Square area).**

Why: Highest lifetime customer value of any business on this list. The gap between what these clients spend and what they could be spending — for clients who have gone quiet for 60–90 days — is immediately quantifiable and usually surprising to the owner. Lead with: "How many of your clients had their last appointment more than 90 days ago?" Let them do the math. Then: "That's the easiest revenue in your business right now — and none of it requires finding a new client."

## Universal Opener for This Territory

"School's been in for two weeks, summer is winding down, and patients and clients are in back-to-routine mode. The practices and businesses that are fully booked in September are the ones that reached their existing people in August. What does your outreach to your existing client base look like right now?"

**In Southlake specifically:** Lead with ROI clarity, not price. These owners are not price-sensitive. They're time-sensitive and outcome-focused. Speak to the specific dollar number they're leaving on the table.

**In Grapevine:** Lead with the story, then the number. Grapefest timing is a natural opener for any business on or near Main Street.

**In Colleyville:** Middle ground — business-owner language, specific problem, specific fix. Don't start with tech. Start with the specific patient or customer who isn't coming back.
