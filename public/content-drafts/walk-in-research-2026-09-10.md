# Walk-In Research — Thursday, September 10, 2026
## Territory: Frisco / McKinney / Prosper

High-growth North Dallas suburbs. Frisco and McKinney are two of the fastest-growing cities in the country — both have exploded with owner-operated businesses serving a dense, affluent, younger-skewing population. Prosper is smaller and newer, but its income profile is among the highest in DFW. This corridor hasn't been in the recent walk-in cycle and represents some of the best-qualified businesses in the metro.

**Suggested route:** Frisco (Preston Road / Main Street / Legacy Drive corridor) 9:30–11:30am → McKinney (Virginia/Central Expressway corridor, downtown McKinney square) 11:30am–1pm → Prosper (Preston Road/US-380 commercial strip) 1–2:30pm.

**Entry line for today's territory:** "I work with owner-operated businesses in Frisco and McKinney. The businesses I've talked to in this area all have one thing in common — strong customer relationships, solid word of mouth, but nothing that systematically works the existing customer list between appointments. That's where the revenue is hiding. What does your follow-up look like right now?"

## Target Businesses

### 1. Independent Med Spa or Aesthetics Clinic — Frisco Preston Road or Legacy corridor
**What they do:** Injectables, laser, body contouring, facials. Frisco has a dense concentration of med spas serving the 30–55 female demographic in high-income ZIP codes — 75034 and 75035 are among the highest-spending aesthetics markets in Texas.
**What to notice:** Check how many Google reviews the practice has. Under 80 is almost always a practice that's doing good work but has no ask system. Also check Instagram — if they're posting 3x per week but have no online booking button in the bio, they're getting interest they're not converting. The core pitch: injectable clients are due back every 3–4 months; how many are being proactively contacted at that interval vs. waiting for the patient to remember to call?
**Hook:** "Your Botox clients have a 3 to 4 month reappointment window. Are they getting a message at that interval, or is the reappointment mostly inbound when they remember to call? Because the ones who don't get a message at 90 days are the ones searching Instagram for a competitor at day 100."

### 2. Independent Pediatric or Family Dental Practice — Frisco or McKinney
**What they do:** General dentistry, pediatric dentistry, cosmetic add-ons. The Frisco-McKinney corridor has hundreds of young families — lots of kids in braces, lots of adults with dental benefits they're not using. 
**What to notice:** December 31 dental benefit deadline is now 112 days away. Any practice that hasn't started its Q4 benefit-expiration recall campaign is leaving money on the table. Ask how they're contacting patients with benefits expiring December 31 — postcards, email, text, or nothing?
**Hook:** "Your patients have dental benefits that expire December 31 — 112 days from now. How many of those patients haven't been in for 12 months and still have benefits available? That's your warmest list. If you're sending postcards, I can show you what a text-based recall sequence gets you versus that."

---

### 3. Independent Chiropractic or Physical Therapy Practice — McKinney or Frisco
**What they do:** Orthopedic rehab, sports medicine, spine and musculoskeletal care. McKinney has a massive athletic population — one of the largest high school districts in Texas runs here, and youth travel sports are everywhere.
**What to notice:** Fall sports injuries are peaking right now — football, volleyball, cross country, soccer, baseball tourneys running year-round. Any practice that has capacity should be reaching its discharged spring/summer patients right now, before those patients search Google when they get hurt again. Ask how patients are notified when they complete a care plan.
**Hook:** "Your patients who finished a care plan in spring are back in fall sports right now. They know your practice, they've seen results, and they're the most likely people in your market to need care again. Is anything reaching out to them before they search for a clinic when the next injury happens?"

---

### 4. Independent Veterinary Clinic — Frisco or Prosper
**What they do:** Small animal veterinary care. Frisco has among the highest dog ownership rates in DFW — the suburb demographics (young families, high income, lots of space) drive a dense pet population. Several independent practices compete with the corporate chains.
**What to notice:** The annual wellness visit is the core recurring revenue event for veterinary practices. A dog or cat that came in for vaccines last October is due again this month. If the practice isn't sending a proactive recall message at the 11-month mark — before the owner forgets and lets it slip to 14 months — they're losing that appointment to a chain with a better reminder system.
**Hook:** "Your patients who came in for their annual exam last October are due right now. Are they getting a reminder at 11 months, or are you waiting for the owner to remember and call? The practices that win the annual exam keep the pet owner between visits too — is anything reaching those clients proactively?"

---

### 5. Independent Fitness Studio or Boutique Gym — Frisco or McKinney
**What they do:** Group fitness, personal training, cycling, pilates, strength classes. The corridor has a high density of boutique studios — Orange Theory, F45, and independents all compete here.
**What to notice:** September is the fitness studio's second-best new membership month of the year (January is first). Back-to-school families are getting back to their routines, kids' schedules are set, and the summer drift is over. Independent studios need two things: a reactivation campaign for members who paused in the summer, and a trial-to-member conversion push for anyone who did a trial class in August. Ask if either is happening.
**Hook:** "How many members paused over the summer? And how many trial classes did you do in August? Those two lists — paused members and trial-class contacts — are your warmest leads right now. Is anything reaching out to them, or is membership growth mostly inbound from your social media?"

---

### 6. Independent Insurance Agency — McKinney or Frisco commercial district
**What they do:** Personal lines and commercial insurance. Frisco and McKinney have a lot of independent agencies — one to three producers, owner-operated, competing against the Allstate and State Farm franchise model.
**What to notice:** Independent agencies have a customer book and a referral opportunity that almost nobody is systematically working. A client who's been happy for 3 years is the best referral source in the building — but most agencies only talk to clients at renewal. Ask what's in place to ask for referrals from satisfied clients at the right moment.
**Hook:** "Your clients who've been with you for 3 or more years are your most credible referral source. Is anything asking them for a referral at the right moment — right after a claim is handled well, right after a renewal goes smoothly — or does new business mostly come from cold inbound calls?"

---

### 7. Independent Auto Detailing Shop — Frisco or McKinney
**What they do:** Interior and exterior car detailing, paint protection, ceramic coating. The Frisco-McKinney corridor has high income and high car value — lots of trucks, SUVs, and luxury vehicles that owners care about keeping clean.
**What to notice:** Fall is when people start thinking about protecting their vehicles after a summer of road trips, heat, and summer storms. If a detailing shop has done work for customers in the spring or early summer, the fall return booking window is open right now. Ask how they stay in contact with past customers between bookings.
**Hook:** "A customer who had their truck detailed in April is thinking about it again right now with fall coming and the summer behind them. Is anything reaching out to your past customers at the 5 to 6 month mark, or does the return booking mostly happen when they happen to notice their car is dirty?"

---

### 8. Independent Orthodontics Practice — Frisco or McKinney
**What they do:** Braces, Invisalign, retainers. The family density in this corridor is extremely high — Frisco ISD alone enrolls 75,000+ students. An independent orthodontist competing with the corporate DSO chains needs to be working the sibling referral and the discharged patient retainer-check window.
**What to notice:** A patient who finished treatment and was told to come back for a retainer check in 6 months is a warm reactivation target. If the practice doesn't have an automated message going out at the 5.5-month mark, they're relying on the patient to remember. Also ask about new patient volume from sibling referrals — most practices get them passively; few ask for them.
**Hook:** "Your finished patients have siblings, and their parents trust you already. Is anything systematically reaching out to former patients about sibling evaluations, or does that new patient pipeline depend on parents thinking to bring the next kid in on their own?"

---

### 9. Independent Hair Salon or Barbershop — McKinney Historic Downtown
**What they do:** Cuts, color, men's grooming. McKinney's historic downtown is a walkable commercial district with high foot traffic and a mix of boutique salons and independent barbershops. Owner density is high.
**What to notice:** Color clients are due back every 6–8 weeks. If a salon has 50 color clients and doesn't have an automated rebooking message going out at the 7-week mark, it's losing bookings to clients who just book whoever is available when they finally think about it. Also look at Google reviews — McKinney salons in the downtown area often have thin review counts relative to their quality.
**Hook:** "Your color clients are on a 6 to 8 week cycle. What percentage of them rebook before they leave? And the ones who don't — is anything reaching out to them at week 7, or are you waiting for them to call when they're ready? Because week 7 is when they're booking with someone else."

### 10. Independent HVAC Company — McKinney or Frisco
**What they do:** Residential HVAC service, repair, and replacement. One of the most active service categories in North Texas — the heat index data and the age of housing stock both create constant demand.
**What to notice:** Fall tune-up season is starting now. The same customers who had summer service in June or July are the natural targets for a fall system check. An HVAC company that doesn't proactively reach out to its summer service customers for fall tune-ups is letting the relationship idle for 4 months and then competing cold for furnace calls in December. Ask what fall outreach looks like.
**Hook:** "Your summer service customers are the warmest leads you have for a fall tune-up. When's the last time someone reached out to that list proactively? Most HVAC companies in this market wait for the furnace call in December — the ones that send a fall check-in in September fill the slower weeks between summer AC service and winter heating calls."

## Best Stops Today

**Priority 1 — Independent dental practice, Frisco or McKinney.** The December 31 dental benefit hook is 112 days out. Any family dental or pediatric practice that doesn't have an active Q4 benefit recall running is leaving money on the table. Frisco and McKinney both have dense concentrations of these practices in strip centers on the main corridors.

**Priority 2 — Med spa or aesthetics clinic on the Preston Road corridor in Frisco.** The income profile and demographic in this ZIP code is exceptional for aesthetics. An independent med spa competing against corporate chains and chains like European Wax is often better positioned on quality and already has a loyal patient base — they just need the follow-up system to work the existing base at the right intervals.

**Priority 3 — McKinney Historic Downtown.** The concentration of owner-operated businesses in the downtown square is unusually high. Salons, boutiques, fitness studios, a few health practices. The owners are present, the foot traffic is real, and the jaded-toward-vendor-pitches vibe that plagues Plano and Frisco corridor stops is lower here. Spend an hour walking the square before heading to Prosper.

## Bonus Observation

Frisco and McKinney are unusual in the DFW walk-in landscape: the business owners are generally well-capitalized, not running scared on margins, and already investing in marketing. The pitch isn't "save money" — it's "capture the revenue you're already earning but losing in the gap between appointments." That framing lands differently with an owner who's making payroll comfortably than with one who's struggling. Lead with the revenue recovery angle, not the efficiency angle.

Prosper is worth 30–45 minutes at the US-380/Preston intersection commercial development. The businesses are newer, the owners are building from scratch in a newer market, and they're less likely to be locked in with a competitor already.
