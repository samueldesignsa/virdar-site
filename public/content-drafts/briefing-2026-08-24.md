## One Insight

The audiology research surfaced something worth naming as a pitch principle: **the aging asset gap**.

Most of Beverly's prospects have a version of this. A practice or business owns a relationship with a customer, and sitting inside that relationship is an asset that's degraded without the customer knowing — and without the business doing anything about it.

For the audiologist: 5-year-old hearing aids the patient thinks are "fine" but that are quietly failing in background noise.

For the dentist: a treatment plan recommended 18 months ago that the patient agreed to but never scheduled.

For the chiropractor: a patient whose insurance coverage renewed in January but whose condition from last spring was never fully addressed.

For the HVAC company: an 8-year-old system in a customer's house that's running fine until it isn't.

For the auto shop: a transmission fluid that should have been changed 8,000 miles ago.

The business already has all the information they need to identify this customer and start the conversation. The patient already has a relationship and a degree of trust. The conversation is easy to have — in most cases it's just "here's what I noticed and what I'd recommend." What's missing is the system that identifies the asset, times the message, and makes the scheduling step easy.

This framing works in every cold conversation Beverly has. "What are the things your customers own that are aging or due for attention — and do you have a system that catches that before the customer finds out on their own?"
