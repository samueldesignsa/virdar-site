# Walk-In Research — Friday, August 28, 2026
## Territory: Allen / Murphy / Wylie (East Collin County)

**Why this territory today:**
East Collin County is a growth corridor that's been largely absent from the recent walk-in cycle (McKinney was Aug 20, Frisco/Prosper was Aug 19 — this is east of both). Allen is one of the fastest-growing cities in North Texas: established residential base, dense strip-mall service corridor along US-75 and Exchange Pkwy, lots of businesses that opened 5–10 years ago with growing customer lists they have no system to work. Murphy and Wylie are smaller, newer, and owner-operated — a very different buyer profile from yesterday's Southlake.

**Drive time from central DFW:** 30–40 minutes north on US-75. Park near Exchange Pkwy / Bethany Dr in Allen for the walk. Drive east to Murphy/Wylie for the afternoon.

**Market framing:** Allen is middle-to-upper-income, well-educated, and skeptical of vendors who can't explain what they're selling clearly. Murphy and Wylie are more blue-collar/middle-income — they want to know if it works, what it costs, and whether they have to manage it. Lead with the specific problem in both markets, but in Allen frame it around the revenue opportunity ("you have 400 patients who haven't been in since January — that's a specific number, and here's what it costs you"). In Wylie and Murphy, lead with the problem they're living with today: "Who's following up with the person who called on Saturday and didn't book?"

**Best timing:** Allen morning (businesses are fresh, no lunch rush). Murphy/Wylie early afternoon.

## 8 Targets

### 1. Allen Chiropractic or Spine Center (Exchange Pkwy or US-75 service road, Allen)
**What they do:** Musculoskeletal care, mix of personal injury and wellness patients. Likely 1–2 providers, front desk handling scheduling.

**Specific observation:** Fall is a strong chiropractic season in DFW — back-to-school posture, sports injuries starting in August, and end-of-year insurance benefits driving patients back in. Independent chiro practices that run a benefits-expiration message in September to their lapsed patient list typically recover 12–18 patients who would otherwise wait until January. Practices that don't reach out lose those patients to whoever reminds them first.

**Hook:** "How many of your patients came in for treatment in the first half of the year and haven't been back? That list has a dollar value attached to it — and most of it is recoverable in September before benefits expire."

### 2. Allen Family Dentistry or Independent Dental Group (Bethany Dr corridor, Allen)
**What they do:** General dentistry, family practice. Established patient base of Allen residents.

**Specific observation:** The September dental recall window is the highest-value period of the year for independent practices. Patients who were seen in Q1 are 6–7 months out. Insurance benefits expire December 31 and most patients don't realize they're leaving money on the table until someone tells them. Practices that send a specific message ("Your last cleaning was in February — you have $X left on your plan and we have slots open in September") fill those slots. Practices that wait for patients to self-schedule lose September revenue.

**Hook:** "Pull your patient list — how many cleanings did you do in January and February? Because those patients are due right now, and if you haven't sent them a specific message yet, your September schedule has openings that shouldn't be there."

### 3. Allen or Murphy Pediatric Practice (any independent peds office)
**What they do:** Pediatric primary care. Heavy August volume from school physicals, starting to tail off into September.

**Specific observation:** The school physical rush is almost over, but there's always a significant tail — kids in year-round schools, families who moved during the summer, kids whose sports eligibility paperwork came in late. Pediatricians who identify that tail proactively (a specific list of patients overdue for annual well-child visits) and send a targeted message in September recover 20–30 of those visits before the October lull. Practices that don't identify the tail just watch September slow down.

**Hook:** "School's been in for two weeks. How many kids in your practice are still overdue for a sports physical or well-child visit? That's a specific list — it's not a mailing, it's targeted outreach to families you already have a relationship with."

### 4. Allen or Murphy Dermatology / Skin Care Practice
**What they do:** Medical and cosmetic dermatology. Mix of skin checks and cosmetic treatments (Botox, laser, filler).

**Specific observation:** Late August is the beginning of fall cosmetic season — summer damage is visible, holiday prep is starting, and the Botox/filler refresh cycle is peaking for patients who were treated in spring. Dermatology practices that proactively reach their spring cosmetic patients with a fall message ("Summer's done a number on your skin — this is the best time for a skin check and to refresh your spring treatment before the holidays") fill September and October cosmetic slots in two days. Practices that don't do that outreach fill those same slots by late October but miss six weeks of revenue.

**Hook:** "Your spring cosmetic patients are all due for a refresh right now. The practices that fill September send a specific message to those patients in August. Have yours gotten that message?"

### 5. Allen Fitness Studio or Pilates / Barre Studio (Exchange Pkwy area)
**What they do:** Group fitness classes — yoga, Pilates, barre, cycling. Membership or class-pack model.

**Specific observation:** Fall is the second-largest enrollment window of the year for fitness studios, after January. Back-to-school creates a routine shift for parents — kids are in school, mornings free up, and the "September reset" motivation is real. Studios that reach their lapsed member list (people who bought a class pack in spring and haven't been back) in the last week of August convert at significantly higher rates than studios that wait for September walk-in traffic. The message is simple: "Your schedule just opened up — here's a link to get back in with us this fall."

**Hook:** "How many people bought a membership or class pack from you in the spring and haven't been back? That's your highest-conversion reactivation list of the year — and right now is the exact right moment to reach them."

### 7. Wylie or Murphy Veterinary Clinic (any independent vet)
**What they do:** Small animal veterinary care, preventive and sick visits. Mix of established clients and new clients from the area's growth.

**Specific observation:** Late summer is prime recall season for independent vets — annual wellness exams, heartworm tests, fall vaccines. Practices that run a proactive September recall campaign (identifying every patient whose annual exam is overdue and sending a specific message) typically recover 20–40 exams they would have lost to drift. Practices that wait for clients to self-schedule lose those visits to competitors or see them lapse entirely. Murphy and Wylie have grown substantially and have a high number of pet-owning households — vet practices there have large recall lists that aren't being fully worked.

**Hook:** "How many pets in your practice are overdue for their annual exam right now? That's not a rhetorical question — that's a specific list in your software, and every one of those patients is either going to get a message from you or drift to whoever reaches them first."

### 8. Allen Insurance Agency (any independent agency on US-75 or Exchange Pkwy)
**What they do:** Personal and commercial insurance — auto, home, life, business. Relationship-based renewal model.

**Specific observation:** Independent insurance agencies are sitting on renewal anniversaries that go unworked. The agencies with the highest retention aren't waiting for renewal dates to remind clients — they're reaching out 60–90 days ahead with a "quick review before your renewal" message that keeps the relationship warm and surfaces cross-sell opportunities (bundling, life coverage, commercial policies) before the client shops around. Agencies that only reach clients at renewal often find those clients have already gotten quotes elsewhere.

**Hook:** "What's your outreach look like for clients who are 60 days from renewal? Because the agency that calls first has a significant retention advantage over the one that sends the standard renewal notice two weeks out. Are you in front of them before they shop?"
