## One Insight

The chiropractic practice has a structural revenue problem that's almost invisible from inside the practice: the business model rewards acute care delivery and does not automatically retain the patients it successfully treated. A chiropractor who is good at their job — who resolves acute pain in 4–5 visits — creates an exit condition. The patient who no longer hurts has no immediate reason to continue. The practice that can bridge that moment with a specific, clinical follow-up ("here's why we continue even though you're not hurting") retains 20–30% more patients through the corrective phase and converts a meaningful subset to maintenance. That's not a sales sequence — it's a clinical handoff that got dropped.

The reason this matters for Beverly's walk-in today is that chiropractic practice owners are clinically trained and often skeptical of anything that sounds like sales. The treatment plan follow-up sequence should be framed as exactly what it is: the automated delivery of the clinical recommendation the chiropractor already makes in the exam room. The doctor told the patient to continue. The system just makes sure the message doesn't get lost between the adjustment table and the parking lot.
