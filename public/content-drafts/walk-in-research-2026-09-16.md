# Walk-In Research — Wednesday, September 16, 2026
## Territory: Southlake / Grapevine / Colleyville

Tarrant County's high-income mid-cities corridor. This area sits between DFW Airport and the Westlake/Keller growth zone — upscale residential, corporate office parks, and strip centers full of owner-operated professional services, medical practices, and premium home services. The demographic here is high-household-income homeowners and dual-income professionals who are not price-sensitive but are time-sensitive.

The businesses in this corridor are different from the east Dallas County and south Dallas County targets. These owners are often busier and harder to reach during work hours — but when you get in front of them, the deal size is larger, the CRM need is real, and the concept of "automation that runs while I'm not here" resonates immediately. They know what an automated system does in their industry (many have tried SaaS tools). What they haven't had is someone build a system specifically for their customer base.

**Suggested route:** Southlake (Southlake Town Square / Central Market corridor) 9:30–11am → Grapevine (Main Street / Northwest Highway commercial strip) 11:15am–12:45pm → Colleyville (Glade Road / Precinct Line Road strip centers) 1–2:30pm.

**Entry line for today's territory:** "I work with owner-operated businesses in Tarrant County. Most of the owners I've talked to in this area are doing strong volume and have a loyal customer base — but nothing is actively following up with past customers or working the pipeline between jobs. ## Target Businesses

**What they do:** Premium residential irrigation installation, service, and landscape lighting for the Southlake/Colleyville luxury home market. Properties in this corridor average significantly larger than standard DFW residential — half-acre and full-acre lots with complex irrigation zones.
**What to notice:** Fall blowout season is live right now. Residential irrigation companies in this market have large per-customer contracts and past customers who had spring startups in March and April but haven't scheduled fall blowouts. **Hook:** "Your spring startup customers need fall blowouts before November. If they're not already scheduled, they'll panic-call in late October when you're already booked out. ## Terrain Note

Southlake Town Square is a walkable mixed-use development with ground-floor professional services, boutique retail, and restaurants. The best target types — aesthetic practices, orthodontists, financial advisors — are in the second-floor office suites above the retail strip. The ground level is primarily retail. Budget 30 minutes to work the Town Square office buildings.

Grapevine's Main Street corridor has independent businesses mixed with tourist-oriented retail. Northwest Highway (TX-114 Business) is the better commercial strip — auto services, medical offices, professional services mixed with national brands. The independent businesses here are less polished than Southlake but just as operational.

Colleyville's commercial concentration is at Glade Road and Precinct Line Road — strip centers with dentists, chiropractors, physical therapists, insurance agencies, and specialty services. Lower foot traffic than Southlake, but owners are more available during business hours because the customers aren't constant. Good territory for longer conversations.

Drive time Southlake Town Square → Grapevine: 10 minutes. Grapevine → Colleyville: 12 minutes. Mid-morning traffic is manageable on all three routes.
