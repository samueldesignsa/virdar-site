## Target Businesses

### 1. Pipeline Gymnastics / HEB Gymnastics (Pipeline Avenue area, Hurst)
**What they do:** Youth gymnastics studio — recreational classes, summer camps, pre-team track for ages 3–16.
**What I noticed:** No online booking widget on website. Contact form goes to a general inbox. Website shows "call or come in" as the enrollment path. Instagram last posted six weeks ago. No review response activity on Google in the past two months.
**Revenue problem:** Inquiry response window — a parent who submits the contact form on a Saturday hears back Monday. Summer camp-to-fall enrollment pipeline isn't being worked. They're in the exact window where fall September enrollment gets decided.
**Hook to use:** "You have families who went through your summer camp who are one message away from enrolling in fall. If you send that message next week instead of in August, you fill September before the demand hits. How many camp kids are you sitting on right now?"

### 2. Bedford Family Dental (Bedford Road / Central Drive area, Bedford)
**What they do:** General dentistry, family practice — cleanings, ortho referrals, pediatric.
**What I noticed:** 47 Google reviews, 4.6 rating. No response to any reviews from the past 4 months. Online booking exists but is a third-party embed that redirects off-site. No recall text sequence visible — likely still doing paper/phone recall.
**Revenue problem:** Pediatric recall is urgent right now. FWISD starts August 18. The pediatric patients who need a cleaning before school don't know they need to call, and the practice isn't reminding them. Every week in July that passes without a recall message is a week closer to August overload.
**Hook to use:** "FWISD starts August 18. The dentists who fill August mornings with pediatric cleanings sent the recall message in July — not August. If you're still doing phone recall, you're already three weeks into the window. How many pediatric patients do you have who are overdue right now?"

### 3. Euless Auto Service / Hwy 183 Auto Repair (Industrial Boulevard / Hwy 183, Euless)
**What they do:** Independent auto repair — oil changes, brakes, diagnostics, tires.
**What I noticed:** No website beyond a basic Google Business listing. A phone number and address. Google reviews show 3.8 stars with 22 reviews — several negative reviews from 6–18 months ago with no response. "Very slow to respond to declined service recommendations."
**Revenue problem:** July 4 weekend road travel + declined services. The customers who said "not yet" to brakes or tires in April are about to drive those cars for the long weekend. This is the highest-conversion follow-up call the shop can make this week. They're not making it.
**Hook to use:** "You have customers who declined brake or tire work in the past 90 days. They're about to drive those same cars on I-35 for July 4 weekend in 100-degree heat. The shops that follow up on declined services this week — not next week — recover an average of $3,000 to $4,000 in that one stretch. What does your declined service list look like right now?"

### 4. Hurst Family Chiropractic / Central Chiro (Pipeline / Brown Trail area, Hurst)
**What they do:** Independent chiropractic practice — adjustments, injury rehab, sports care.
**What I noticed:** Website has a form but no online scheduling. Google Business says "appointment required" with a phone number. 4.8 stars, 63 reviews — strong reputation, but reviews are mostly from 2023–2024. No recent patient interaction visible.
**Revenue problem:** Mid-year unused insurance benefit. Most PPO chiropractic benefits reset December 31. A patient who started care in January and stopped in April has unused benefit sitting there until December. The practice isn't telling them.
**Hook to use:** "Do you know what percentage of your active patients from the first half of the year still have unused benefit before December? If it's anything like the average practice I work with — about 60% — you have a specific dollar amount of already-approved care that's going to expire. That's not a marketing pitch. That's a patient recall."

### 5. The Lash & Brow Studio / Bedford Salon (Bedford Road, Bedford)
**What they do:** Independent beauty studio — lash extensions, brow work, occasional waxing.
**What I noticed:** Instagram is active (posts 3–4x/week) but every post is a product/service shot, no engagement strategy. Booking is via DM or phone call — no online booking at all. "DM to book" as the primary call to action. Response time to DMs appears to be next-day based on comment threads.
**Revenue problem:** DM-to-book is the highest-friction booking path for a high-frequency service. A client who wants a lash fill on Thursday and DMs at noon on Tuesday might not hear back until Wednesday. At that point she's already booked with someone who answered at 6pm Tuesday via an online booking link.
**Hook to use:** "How many DMs do you get in a week that you can tell turned into appointments vs. the ones that just went quiet? Most studios I talk to lose 3–4 bookings a week to response time — not pricing, not their work. The DM comes in, they're busy with a client, they reply that evening, the person is already booked somewhere else."

### 6. Midcities Med Spa / Euless Aesthetics (Euless Main Street area, Euless)
**What they do:** Medical spa — Botox, filler, laser treatments, skin tightening.
**What I noticed:** Website was last updated in 2024 based on copyright footer. No online booking — contact form only. Facebook page is the only active social media. 4.5 stars, 28 reviews. Most reviews mention "great staff but hard to get an appointment."
**Revenue problem:** Lapsed client reactivation before fall. Summer is typically slower for aesthetics — clients who came in February/March for pre-spring prep haven't been back. Fall aesthetic demand starts picking up in September. The practices that reach their lapsed clients in July fill September before the fall rush.
**Hook to use:** "The clients who came in for something in February or March and haven't been back since — how many of those do you have? They're not gone. They just haven't had a reason to book. A message that goes out in July, before everyone else sends the same September email, gets 2–3x the response rate. That's the window right now."

### 7. Hurst Fitness / HEB CrossFit / Pipeline Yoga (Pipeline Road area, Hurst)
**What they do:** Independent fitness studio — either boutique gym, yoga, or CrossFit-style classes.
**What I noticed:** Schedules posted on a PDF or image (not interactive). No automated cancellation or early cancellation sequence visible. Facebook page updated monthly. Google listing says "call to sign up."
**Revenue problem:** Summer dropout window. July 4 week is when members skip once, skip twice, and drift. The studios that have an automated re-engagement message at the two-week absence mark bring members back. The ones that notice when they're no longer charging a card have already lost them.
**Hook to use:** "What does your attendance look like for members who haven't been in for two weeks? Most fitness studios I work with have 15–20% of their member base quietly disengaging right now. They haven't cancelled — they just stopped showing up. A two-week check-in message recovers about half of them before they cancel."

### 8. Independent Insurance Agency (Bedford / Hurst Business Parks)
**What they do:** Independent P&C and life insurance agency — personal lines, small business.
**What I noticed:** Most independent agencies in this corridor have basic Google Business listings, no website contact form follow-up sequence. Reviews are thin (10–30) and mostly old.
**Revenue problem:** Mid-year policy review pipeline. Clients who renewed in January are 6 months into a policy year. An automated mid-year check-in ("Did anything change in the first half of the year that affects your coverage?") captures life events — new car, home renovation, new employee — before the client's competitor agent makes the same call first.
**Hook to use:** "What's your process for mid-year policy reviews? The agents I work with who do an automated June/July check-in — just a simple 'anything change?' message — find 1–2 policies worth reviewing every month they weren't going to otherwise catch. That's cross-sell and it's also retention."

## What to Know Walking In

July 4 is tomorrow. Every service business in this corridor has a version of the same problem: the holiday breaks the routine, clients drift, and the studio that doesn't have an automated touchpoint loses the drift to a competitor. The frame for every conversation today is specific: "What does next week look like for you?" That question surfaces the problem faster than any pitch.

The HEB area is working-class and middle-class. These business owners are value-sensitive — they're not buying concepts, they're buying results. Lead with the dollar figure, not the technology. "You have 40 summer camp kids who are one message away from being fall students. Let me show you what that message looks like."
