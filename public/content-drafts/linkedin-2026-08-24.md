**Topic:** The patients wearing five-year-old hearing aids nobody has called.

**Post:**

---

The average independent audiology practice has 70 to 90 patients wearing hearing aids that are more than five years old.

Those devices are a generation behind on background noise processing, Bluetooth connectivity, and rechargeable technology. Most of those patients would upgrade if they knew what was available.

None of them have been told.

There's no flag in the practice management software that says "device aging — start upgrade conversation." The aids age out quietly. The patient shows up when something breaks, frustrated, and the upgrade happens at the worst possible moment instead of a planned one.

A practice that reaches those 80 patients this fall — before December benefit deadlines — with a specific message ("Your aids are from 2021. Technology has changed significantly. We'd like to show you the difference") is looking at 20 to 25 demonstration appointments.

At $4,200 per pair, that's a significant revenue window sitting in an existing patient panel.

The patients already trust the practice. The devices are already old. The insurance benefits expire in four months.

All that's missing is the message.
