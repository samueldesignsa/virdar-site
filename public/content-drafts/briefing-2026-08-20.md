## One Insight

The allergy blog surfaced something that applies more broadly than allergy practices: **the most valuable patient a practice has is the one it already treated.**

A lapsed allergy patient has been seen before, already trusts the practice, and is 12 days from being symptomatic again. The practice's cost to re-acquire that patient is zero — no ads, no referral fee, no SEO spend. A text message that says "ragweed season starts soon, we have appointments available" converts at 11 percent versus 4 percent organic. That 7 percentage point difference across 900 lapsed patients is 63 additional appointments, at zero acquisition cost.

The same math applies at every practice Beverly walks into today. The chiropractic patient who came in for an acute injury three months ago and never scheduled maintenance. The dental patient who used their benefits in February and hasn't been back. The vet client whose dog is due for an annual wellness exam. All of them are patients/clients the practice already paid to acquire — through word of mouth, through a referral, through years of building a local reputation. The practices that re-engage them systematically are not running a marketing campaign. They're collecting on a relationship that already exists.

This is the simplest framing for any walk-in conversation today: "You've already done the hard part — you've treated these people. They liked you well enough to come in. The ones who aren't coming back aren't gone because they chose a competitor. Most of them just haven't heard from you. What would it take to send one message this week to everyone who hasn't been in since spring?"
