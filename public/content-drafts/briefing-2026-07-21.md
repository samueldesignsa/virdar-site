The pediatric PT IEP angle introduced this shift is a new category of conversation for Beverly — one that doesn't start with "here's what your business is losing" but instead starts with "here's a referral pipeline that exists and you're not in it." Most of Beverly's walk-in conversations to date have focused on operational gaps (the dry cleaner who doesn't notify customers, the dentist who doesn't recall overdue patients). The IEP angle is different: it's not about fixing something broken. It's about tapping something invisible.

That distinction matters in how Beverly frames it. The dry cleaner conversation starts with a problem ("how many calls are you getting asking 'is my order ready?'"). The pediatric PT conversation starts with an absence ("how many special education coordinators within 5 miles of your practice know your name?"). The absence is less emotionally immediate than the problem — the dry cleaner feels the status calls every day; the pediatric PT doesn't feel the IEP referrals they're not receiving, because they don't know they exist. The conversation Beverly needs to have is the one that makes the invisible visible: "September is when school districts start referring families to outside PT providers for IEP services. The practices that introduced themselves in August are already on the list. You have 28 days."

Once the owner understands that a specific referral pipeline opens in 28 days and that the cost of being on the list is one afternoon and one letter — the automation that manages the referral follow-up, the authorization communication, the appointment scheduling — is not the pitch. It's the obvious next step.
