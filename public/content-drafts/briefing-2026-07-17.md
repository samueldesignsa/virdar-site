## One Insight

Urgent care is the most underutilized lead context in Beverly's walk-in territory. The pitch to an urgent care owner is distinct from the pitch to a dental or orthodontic practice — it's not about building long-term patient relationships, because urgent care is inherently transactional. The pitch is about transaction volume: capturing walk-ins who are already standing outside (queue abandonment), converting existing patients into return patients (post-visit follow-up), and making the practice visible to the patients who are searching right now (sports physical marketing, Google review velocity).

The urgent care owner who's busy in July has a specific blind spot: they're measuring success by how full the waiting room is today, not by how many patients they lost between visits. The patient who came in February for strep, never got a check-in, and went somewhere else for their UTI in June is invisible in the day's revenue numbers. But they're $175 that went to a competitor, plus the annual visit value that now belongs to whoever they found in June. A practice doing 38 visits per day with a 28% 12-month return rate that could have a 38% return rate is leaving $200K on the floor annually — from the same patient volume, the same location, the same providers.

That's the frame for the urgent care conversation: your waiting room is full today. Your question is whether the patients walking out today come back to you next time, or go to Google. The answer is almost entirely a function of whether you sent them a message 24 hours after their visit.
