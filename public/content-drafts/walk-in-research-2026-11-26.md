# Walk-In Research — Thursday, November 26, 2026 (Thanksgiving)

**For use: Friday, November 27, 2026 — Black Friday**
**Area: Downtown Plano Arts District + Preston Road Corridor**

---

## Why This Area on Black Friday

Downtown Plano is a walkable historic district with the highest concentration of independent businesses in Collin County. On Black Friday, it draws a different crowd than the mall corridors — people specifically looking for local, independent options. Business owners are in-store because it's one of their better revenue days, but it's not wall-to-wall chaos like Stonebriar or Legacy West. You can have a real conversation.

The area spans about a 10-minute walk from 15th Street down to the Haggard Farm complex. Park once near E 15th Street and K Ave and work the strip on foot.

## Businesses to Visit

### 1. 1418 Coffee
**Address:** 1418 F Street, Downtown Plano  
**What they do:** Specialty coffee shop in a historic building in the Downtown Plano Arts District. One of the few Texas coffee shops to appear on Yelp's Top 100 Restaurants list — high quality, real local following.  
**What I noticed:** No evidence of an automated catering inquiry process on their website. Office holiday catering in the downtown Plano corridor — law offices, insurance agencies, creative agencies — is a natural revenue stream they're likely leaving on the floor. No visible gift card purchase link on their Google listing.  
**Best hook:** "With the holiday office party season going right now, are you fielding catering inquiries? What does your follow-up process look like?" The catering angle is natural, timely, and doesn't require any technical explanation.

### 2. Bibliobar
**Address:** 1017 E 15th Street, Downtown Plano  
**What they do:** Independent bookstore + bar, opened March 2025. Owner built it specifically to diversify the retail mix on 15th Street. First holiday season in operation.  
**What I noticed:** This is their first Black Friday and holiday season. No evidence of a gift card campaign, an email list capture at checkout, or a holiday wishlist/pre-order system. Gift cards are massive for indie bookstores — people buy them when they don't know what book to get. They're almost certainly under-capturing this.  
**Best hook:** "It's your first holiday season — congratulations. Are you doing gift cards? What's your plan for capturing people who want to give a bookstore gift but aren't sure what title?" The owner is likely already thinking about this and will appreciate the specific framing.

### 3. Anomalia Beauty Society
**Address:** Downtown Plano (second location, opened spring 2026)  
**What they do:** Latina-owned full-service salon — haircuts, color, balayage, extensions. Second location opened in 2026, so they're in growth mode.  
**What I noticed:** A salon opening a second location has usually outgrown its manual scheduling and follow-up systems. Holiday appointment demand (blowouts, color before parties, extensions for NYE) is peaking right now. No evidence of an automated rebooking sequence or lapsed-client reactivation.  
**Best hook:** "With holiday appointments booking up, are you finding that people book once and then fall off your calendar for 4–5 months? What does your follow-up look like after someone's first appointment?" Rebooking conversion and lapsed reactivation are the two highest-ROI systems for salons.

### 4. Mudleaf Coffee
**Address:** 3100 Independence Pkwy, Suite 300, Plano  
**What they do:** Independent specialty coffee shop, ~300 Yelp reviews at 4.0 stars, known for laid-back atmosphere.  
**What I noticed:** 4.0 stars with 300 reviews is below what a specialty shop with a good following should have. Check their Google listing before you go — if reviews mention consistency issues that go unanswered, that's the opening. Gift card and holiday catering angle also applies.  
**Best hook:** "I noticed you're at 4.0 on Google — do you respond to reviews? I work with coffee shops on a system that gets response time under 24 hours and has measurably moved ratings for similar shops in Collin County." If their reviews are unanswered, this opens itself.

### 5. Ramen Belly
**Address:** Downtown Plano (opened 2025)  
**What they do:** Independent ramen restaurant, first full holiday season in operation.  
**What I noticed:** New restaurant in its first holiday season — almost certainly no automated inquiry response, no holiday catering campaign, no email list beyond whatever they built on Instagram. December private dining and corporate catering bookings are happening right now and they may not have a system to capture them.  
**Best hook:** "Are you doing any private dining or catering for corporate holiday parties? Those bookings are happening in the next two weeks — do you have a process for responding to inquiries fast enough to close them?"

### 6. Local Good Coffee Co.
**Address:** 201 Legacy Drive, Plano  
**What they do:** One of the largest independent coffee shops in North Texas — opened 2025 with specialty coffee, food menu, and pickleball courts. High-profile, well-funded, significant space.  
**What I noticed:** A large new coffee concept like this likely has strong operational systems but may not have an automated loyalty reactivation or catering follow-up process yet. They're in their first full holiday season. Big email list potential given foot traffic volume.  
**Best hook:** "With the pickleball courts, you're drawing a crowd that's different from the typical coffee shop regular — sports leagues, group events, corporate bookings. What does your process look like for capturing those group event inquiries and following up?" Event/group booking automation is the natural angle here.

### 7. Mo's Fiery Chicken & BBQ
**Address:** Downtown Plano (opened 2025)  
**What they do:** Independent restaurant, first holiday season, Downtown Plano.  
**What I noticed:** New restaurant, first holiday season, likely building their catering reputation. December is when catering revenue is made. BBQ/chicken shops are natural office catering options because the food travels well and feeds groups cleanly.  
**Best hook:** "BBQ is one of the easiest sells for office holiday catering because everyone eats it and it's easy to set up. Are you fielding catering inquiries? What does your process look like for getting back to them quickly?"

### 8. Ka-dou
**Address:** 1900 Preston Road, Suite 228, Plano  
**What they do:** Independent women's contemporary clothing boutique on Preston Road.  
**What I noticed:** Boutique clothing is one of the highest-stakes businesses for the holiday season — a bad November/December can determine the entire year's profitability. Gift cards, lapsed customer reactivation, holiday preview events are all peak-ROI systems right now. Likely managing customer outreach manually or not at all.  
**Best hook:** "Black Friday is today — one of your biggest days. What's your plan for staying in touch with the customers who came in today and don't come back until next November?" The timing makes the question land immediately — they're in the middle of exactly the problem.

### 9. Dallas Nails & Lashes
**Address:** 8408 Preston Road, Suite 310, Plano  
**What they do:** Nail salon with lash services on Preston Road.  
**What I noticed:** Nail and lash businesses have high repeat-visit potential but notoriously low rebooking rates. Holiday nail appointments (gel manicures, holiday designs) are peaking now through NYE. No evidence of an automated rebooking or lapsed-client system.  
**Best hook:** "How often do your holiday nail clients come back after Christmas? A lot of salons we work with see a big spike in December and then a cliff in January — is that something you deal with?" The pain point is universal and the question opens the conversation without sounding like a pitch.

### 10. Blue Mango
**Address:** 1033 E 15th Street, Downtown Plano  
**What they do:** Long-running independent Indian restaurant in Downtown Plano, one of the anchor tenants on 15th Street.  
**What I noticed:** Established local restaurant with real foot traffic. Holiday catering — corporate Indian food orders for multicultural office holiday parties — is a genuine revenue stream. Check their Google reviews before visiting; long-running independents often have review response gaps.  
**Best hook:** "Long-running restaurants in the Arts District like yours usually have a loyal following — are you doing anything to reach them before the holidays? Or do you rely mostly on walk-in traffic?" The pivot from loyal following to a system that activates it is a natural conversation for an owner who's been there a while.
