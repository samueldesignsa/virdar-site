# LinkedIn Post — Wednesday, July 29, 2026

**Topic:** The patient who got better. And left.

---

The most expensive moment in a chiropractic practice isn't when a patient never shows up.

It's when a patient gets better — completes 4 or 5 visits, the acute pain is gone — and the practice never hears from them again.

That patient had insurance coverage for 20 more visits through December. They had a structural problem that was addressed symptomatically, not corrected. They'll be back in 8 months with the same issue and a new deductible.

The practice did everything right clinically. It just didn't follow up.

A message at day 7 after the last visit — "You've completed your acute phase. Here's what the corrective phase addresses and why it matters" — is not chasing someone who left. It's a clinical handoff that got dropped.

25% of patients who receive it come back.

The ones who don't will still call when the next episode hits. But the ones who do complete the corrective plan are the ones who become maintenance patients. That's where the stable, predictable revenue actually lives.
