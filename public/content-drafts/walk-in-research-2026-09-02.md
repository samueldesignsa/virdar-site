# Walk-In Research — Wednesday, September 2, 2026
## Territory: Flower Mound / Lewisville / Highland Village, TX

**Why this territory today:**
Flower Mound, Highland Village, and Lewisville form a high-income suburban corridor along FM 2499, Highway 121, and the western shore of Lake Lewisville. Flower Mound consistently ranks in the top 5 wealthiest cities in Texas by household income. Highland Village is boutique — a small, tight community where business owners know each other and word-of-mouth referrals move fast. Lewisville has the commercial density (I-35E/Business 121 corridor) with a mix of long-established independents and newer businesses serving the growth from Flower Mound and Argyle.

This corridor has not been in the recent walk-in cycle. The businesses here serve higher-income households, which means higher average ticket values and customers who respond well to premium service positioning — exactly the frame Virdar fits. AI automation isn't a cost-cutting tool here; it's a capacity and quality-of-service tool. That framing lands with owners in this market.

**Drive time from central DFW:** 30–40 minutes northwest. FM 2499 north from Grapevine, or I-35E north from Carrollton.

**Best timing:** Hit Flower Mound (Long Prairie Road / FM 2499 corridor) first at 9:30–10am. Medical and professional services are best mid-morning. Lewisville's Business 121 / Main Street commercial district is good late morning. Highland Village (Shops at Highland Village) is best 11am–1pm when boutique-facing businesses have staff in and owners are present.

**Market framing for this corridor:** These owners have invested in their physical space, their staff, and their brand. They don't want to hear about "efficiency." They want to hear about keeping up with their customer base, staying in front of the households that chose them, and not losing high-value customers to whoever happened to send a message first. The September timing frame works perfectly here: "Your fall client list is populated. The question is whether you're reaching them before the holiday season picks up speed."

## 10 Targets

### 1. Flower Mound Dental Practice (Long Prairie Road / Cross Timbers Road area)
**What they do:** General or cosmetic dentistry, serving the high-income Flower Mound household base. Practices in this corridor often have above-average revenue per patient and patients who value continuity over convenience.

**Specific observation:** Flower Mound dentists are competing against large DSO chains (Aspen, Western Dental, Brident) that have automated recall systems already running. An independent who doesn't have a systematic recall process is losing patients not to price but to whoever reached the patient first when they were due. September is the peak recall window — January/February patients are overdue, December insurance benefits are on the horizon.

**Hook:** "The big chains in this area have automated recall running every day. Independent practices that don't have the same system are giving patients a reason to go wherever emails them first. How does your six-month recall reach your patients right now?"

### 2. Flower Mound Med Spa or Aesthetics Practice (any independent injector, Long Prairie or FM 2499)
**What they do:** Botox, filler, laser treatments, body contouring — self-pay cosmetic in a market with above-average disposable income. Flower Mound households have the purchasing power for repeat aesthetic visits; the question is whether the practice is reaching them systematically.

**Specific observation:** Spring Botox patients (March–April treatments) are exactly 5–6 months out — at the wear-off window. Holiday season planning starts now. A single September message to spring patients ("Your spring treatment was 5–6 months ago — this is the ideal time for your fall refresh before the holidays") fills October and November before the walk-in holiday traffic starts. Med spas in Flower Mound that run this system book their fall schedule in the first week of September.

**Hook:** "Your March and April patients are all at the wear-off point right now. The practices that fill October and November send that message in September. Has yours gotten it?"

### 3. Lewisville or Flower Mound Orthodontic Practice (any independent ortho on 121 or Long Prairie)
**What they do:** Braces, Invisalign, retainers — pediatric and adult orthodontic care. Back-to-school season is wrapping up; families who were going to start treatment in August and didn't get around to it are still deciding.

**Specific observation:** Orthodontic consultations that stalled in July and August are sitting in most practices' CRM right now — families who came in for a consult, got a treatment plan, and went home to "think about it." School just started; the trigger event is over. The families who don't get a follow-up message in the next two weeks will drift until next summer. A single automated follow-up message to August consultation no-shows recovers 15–20% of them before the back-to-school window closes entirely.

**Hook:** "How many families came in for a consultation in July or August and haven't started treatment yet? Most orthodontic practices we talk to have 20–30 in that bucket. The ones who start in September are the practices that followed up this week."

### 4. Lewisville or Highland Village Chiropractic Practice (any independent office)
**What they do:** Musculoskeletal care — a mix of accident injury, sports recovery, and wellness maintenance. September is one of the two best recall months in chiropractic; fall activity changes create recurring demand from patients who came in earlier in the year.

**Specific observation:** Chiro practices with a large wellness panel — patients who completed care earlier in the year and are now 4–6 months out — have a warm recall list ready right now. A September recall message to lapsed wellness patients recovers 10–20 visits in the first two weeks.

**Hook:** "Your wellness patients who came in March through June — when did they last hear from you? That's your September schedule. How are you reaching that list right now?"

### 5. Lewisville Auto Repair (any established independent on Business 121 or Main Street)
**What they do:** General repair, oil changes, tires, brakes. Lewisville has a dense automotive corridor with long-established independents competing against Firestone, Jiffy Lube, and Midas. The independents win on trust; they lose on follow-up.

**Specific observation:** Auto repair shops know exactly when each vehicle is due for its next service, and almost never communicate that to the customer proactively. The service reminder text — sent 30 days before the expected next visit based on last visit mileage — brings back 20–30% more repeat visits than waiting for the customer to remember. In a market where chains send reminder emails automatically, the independent shop that doesn't is training its customers to forget about them between visits.

**Hook:** "How many customers did you service 4–5 months ago who haven't been back? In most shops, that's 40–60% of the customer base. One reminder message brings most of them back — but only if you send it before they need it, not after they've already gone somewhere else."

### 6. Flower Mound or Highland Village Veterinary Clinic (any independent small animal vet)
**What they do:** Small animal care — wellness exams, vaccines, sick visits, dentals. Flower Mound and Highland Village are pet-dense communities: high household formation, young families with dogs, cats, and sometimes exotics.

**Specific observation:** Annual wellness exam recall is the single highest-value automated system an independent vet can run. September is ideal: summer travel is over, school routines are set, and pet owners are in a "get the important things done" mindset. Independent vets in this market compete against corporate chains (Banfield, BluePearl, VCA) that already run automated recall. The independent who runs systematic recall outperforms the corporate chain on relationship and on availability — but only if the patient hears from them.

**Hook:** "How many pets in your practice are overdue for their annual exam right now? The corporates know that number and are emailing those clients. The independent that reaches their patients first keeps them — but only if they send the message."

### 7. Lewisville or Flower Mound Physical Therapy Practice (any independent outpatient PT)
**What they do:** Outpatient PT — orthopedic, sports, post-surgical rehab. September brings fall sports activity and returning-to-routine patients who re-injure with schedule changes.

**Specific observation:** Independent PT practices have two distinct revenue problems that are both solvable in September. Plan-of-care dropout — patients who stopped coming before they were clinically done — is the most common. Lapsed patient recall — patients discharged 12–18 months ago with recurring conditions — is the most overlooked. September is the right time for both: school sports have started and patients who resolved earlier in the year are beginning to feel the familiar issue again.

**Hook:** "Who in your practice came in earlier this year and discharged before they were really done? That's a list — and September is when those patients start feeling it again. Are you reaching them, or waiting for them to call?"
