## One Insight

The personal training business has a structural retention problem that almost never gets framed as an automation problem — because it looks like a client motivation problem. "The client just lost momentum." But the dropout pattern is entirely predictable: six weeks, package midpoint, the first goal achieved, the summer pause. Every one of those moments has a two-week window where a well-timed message changes the outcome, and every one of them gets missed because the trainer is in sessions.

The relevant thing for Beverly's pitch today is that personal trainers are uniquely receptive to this framing because they already think in systems — exercise programs, progressive overload, periodization. They understand that structure produces results. The pitch is simply: "Your client communication needs the same structure you put into their training program. Right now it's ad hoc. We build the program for the communication side."

That framing lands differently than "we'll automate your follow-up." It speaks the trainer's language.
