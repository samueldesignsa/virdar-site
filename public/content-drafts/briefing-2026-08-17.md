## One Insight

The waxing/esthetics post surfaced something worth putting in Beverly's pitch repertoire: **the series abandonment problem is one of the clearest examples of why "full books" doesn't mean "maximum revenue."**

A studio doing 90 appointments a month, running at 85% capacity, looks healthy. But if 38% of the series they've sold are sitting incomplete — clients bought 6 sessions, used 3, stopped — the studio has already collected that revenue and is losing those clients permanently. The metric that looks healthy (booking volume) is hiding the metric that matters (client retention and LTV).

This applies everywhere Beverly goes. A dental practice can be fully booked and losing 20% of its patient base to drift every year. A fitness studio can be at 80% class capacity while its best members are showing up less and less. A chiropractic practice can be busy with new patient intakes while 40% of treatment plan patients drop out before completion.

The pitch question Beverly can use in any stop: "Your schedule looks pretty full — that's great. But of the clients or patients who came in in January, what percentage are still active with you right now? What's that number — and what's your process for recovering the ones who stopped showing up?"

Most business owners have never thought about that question specifically. They measure their schedule. They don't measure their retention. The ones who measure retention become Beverly's best clients.
